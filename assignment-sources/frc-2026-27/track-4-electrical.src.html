Module 1.1

Handle the battery and power safely

30 min

You will: Move, plug in and charge a robot battery without hurting yourself or the battery.

  1. Wear rated safety glasses, and carry the battery by its case, never by its wires.
  2. Treat both terminals as live: a short can arc at over 500 A. Keep metal tools clear.
  3. Connect only at the SB50, with every terminal fully covered and no bare metal (R607).
  4. Let it cool about 15 min after a match, then charge on a team charger at 6 A or less.
  5. Tag any dropped, cracked or leaking battery bad and tell a mentor; never repair one.

The battery sits secured with its SB50 fully plugged in, and no bare metal shows at any terminal.

More detail
  • Put on safety glasses before any robot work: the FIRST Safety Manual requires rated eyewear.
  • 2026 rule R607: every battery, breaker and terminal connection is fully covered, with no bare metal.
  • The team charger must have a matching SB connector.
  • Set aside a damaged battery, and use the spill kit on any leak.
  • Crimp battery leads or wire main power only in a mentor-supervised group session.

Module 1.2

Tour the 2027 control system

40 min

You will: Point to each part of the robot's power and control system and say what it does.

  1. Trace power with a finger: battery, SB50, 120 A main, PD, breakers, controllers, motors.
  2. Name the PD: REV PDH, CTRE PDP or PDP 2.0, AndyMark AMPD, or ThriftyBot PowerHub.
  3. Find the controller: SystemCore for 2027; the team's 2026 robot still runs a roboRIO.
  4. Find the Vivid VH-109 radio (link to the driver station) and the RSL (signal light).
  5. Trace one yellow and green CAN cable to a motor controller: commands and data, not power.

You can touch each part in order, battery to motor, and say what it does.

More detail
  • Power path in full: battery, SB50, 120 A main breaker, power distribution (PD), branch breakers, motor controllers, motors.
  • The ThriftyBot PowerHub is new (a 2027 preview device).
  • FIRST requires SystemCore for 2027 (built by Limelight on a Raspberry Pi CM5). The roboRIO is now legacy hardware.
  • The radio links the robot to the driver station, and its lights must stay visible. The RSL (robot signal light) flashes when the robot is enabled and stays solid when disabled.
  • The CAN cable runs from the controller to a motor controller. CAN carries commands and data, not motor power.
  • Timing: every team gets a 4 GB SystemCore in the Kickoff Kit (Jan 9, 2027), and FIRST sells them from Nov 18, 2026 ($550, one per team at first).

Module 1.3

Read a robot wiring diagram

40 min

You will: Read a wiring diagram: what each wire carries, its gauge and what protects it.

  1. Open WPILib Intro to FRC Robot Wiring (2026 Kitbot); read SystemCore for its roboRIO.
  2. Read colors: positive red, negative black or blue; CAN-H yellow, CAN-L green.
  3. Read sizes: 6 AWG to the PDH, 12 AWG to each SPARK MAX, 18 AWG for controller, radio.
  4. Read protection: controller and radio each get a 10 A fuse, alone on that branch.
  5. Redraw one motor's circuit by hand: color, gauge and breaker on every wire.

Your drawing shows every wire with a color, a gauge and the fuse or breaker that protects it.

More detail
  • Redraw the circuit from battery to motor. The wiring page shows a roboRIO; read SystemCore in its place.
  • Positive can also be yellow, white, brown, or black with a stripe.
  • The 6 AWG red wire runs from the 120 A breaker to the PDH. The 18 AWG wires power the controller and radio.
  • Each gets its own 10 A fuse. In the Kitbot the controller and radio fuses are PDH channels 20 to 22, with nothing else on that branch.
  • Trace the CAN chain: controller, through each SPARK MAX, ending at the PDH with its TERM switch ON.

Module 2.1

Size wire and breakers to the rules

35 min

You will: Pick the right breaker and the smallest legal wire for any robot circuit.

  1. Size wire to the breaker: the wire must survive whatever current the breaker allows.
  2. Use the 2026 table: 31 to 40 A needs 12 AWG, 21 to 30 A 14 AWG, 6 to 20 A 18 AWG.
  3. Run 6 AWG or thicker for main power: battery, SB50, 120 A main breaker, PD.
  4. Give each motor controller its own 40 A or smaller breaker; a compressor hub 20 A max.
  5. Keep one breaker per circuit, one wire per PD terminal, and each breaker fully seated.

Every circuit on your list has one breaker and a wire at least as thick as the table asks.

More detail
  • Start from the breaker: it sets the wire size, because the wire must survive whatever current the breaker lets through.
  • The 2026 table is the baseline until the Jan 9, 2027 manual. 31 to 40 A needs 12 AWG, 21 to 30 A needs 14 AWG, 6 to 20 A needs 18 AWG.
  • A PH or PCM running a compressor gets 20 A or less.
  • Push every breaker and fuse fully into its PD slot.
  • Leave manufacturer leads as shipped: a Kraken X44's 18 in, 10 AWG leads are legal.
That is not what I see
We only have 10 AWG and the table says 12Thicker (a smaller number) is always fine. Some teams prefer 10 AWG for Krakens.
The motor never pulls 40 A, so can I use 18 AWG on a 40 A breaker?No. Size the wire to the breaker, not to what the motor usually draws.
The nuts won't thread onto the new Littelfuse 120 A main breakerIt has 1/4-28 studs, so standard 1/4-20 nuts do not fit (2027 preview device).

Module 2.2

Crimp, connect and strain-relieve wires

60 min

You will: Make crimped, ferruled and lever-nut connections that pass a pull test and survive robot motion.

  1. Use the right crimper: ratcheting for terminals and Anderson, a ferrule one for ferrules.
  2. Join motor wires with Wago 221 splices or Anderson Powerpole; battery gets the SB50.
  3. Pull-test every connection and look for whiskers; a second person checks any remake.
  4. Tighten bolted terminals so they do not twist; secure wires near connectors with slack.
  5. Add a port saver or pigtail to any plug you unplug often, so the device's plug lasts.

Your sample connections hold a firm pull, show no whiskers, and a teammate checked each.

More detail
  • A ratcheting crimper does terminals and Anderson contacts. Wago 221 are lever splices. The battery uses the pink or red SB50.
  • Whiskers are stray strands. Pull-test every connection by hand.
  • Bolted terminals: battery, main breaker, PD. Tighten so they do not twist by hand.
  • Secure each wire near its connector with a little slack, keep it clear of moving parts, and do not over-tie. Clip or tie the SB50 halves together.
  • A port saver or pigtail keeps the device's own connector from wearing out.
  • 2027 preview: power circuits need COTS connectors, and SystemCore uses Molex SL (CAN, RSL) and Micro-Fit (power). The Kickoff Kit Black Tote has Molex SL cables and parts; custom lengths still need crimping.
That is not what I see
A strand sticks out of my ferruleCut the ferrule off and crimp a new one; a loose strand can touch the next terminal.
A Wago lever came looseSpring connectors work loose over time. Re-seat it, and check every one at least once per event.
The SB50 pulls apart when the robot gets hitClip or zip-tie the two halves together.
Check yourself
Two 18 AWG wires need to reach one PDH terminal. A teammate twists and solders them together so they count as one wire. Legal?

Module 2.3

Wire and terminate a CAN bus

45 min

You will: Wire a CAN bus as one chain terminated at both ends, and plan SystemCore's five buses.

  1. Wire CAN as one daisy chain: yellow CAN-H to yellow, green CAN-L to green.
  2. Terminate both ends with 120 ohms; a PDH with TERM ON can end the far side.
  3. Plan SystemCore's 5 CAN buses (can_s0 to can_s4) by subsystem.
  4. Enable each CAN motor controller by CAN or PWM, never both at once (2026 R714).
  5. Give programmers a list of each device's bus: 2027 code names the bus (a CANPort).

Your bus plan shows each chain, both terminated ends and every device's bus name.

More detail
  • The chain runs device to device.
  • The controller's end has termination built in; the far end needs a terminating device, such as a PDH with its TERM switch ON.
  • Example plan: the drivetrain alone on one bus. Never wire both CAN and PWM enable at once (2026 rule R714).
  • 2027 preview: a CANivore may add an extra bus in 2027 only; from 2028 every bus must start at SystemCore.
  • 2027 code names the bus (a CANPort) for every CAN device.

Module 2.4

Test batteries and log them in the app

30 min

You will: Test a battery with a Battery Beak, decide if it is match-ready, and log it in the Techmen app.

  1. Label each battery for good: team number, year and ID (the app uses labels like B07).
  2. Plug the Battery Beak into the SB50; read resistance, state of charge and voltage.
  3. Judge it: under 0.015 ohm good, over 0.020 ohm consider pulling, 12.5 V resting swap it.
  4. In the Techmen app battery log, tap Log a run, pick the battery, choose What was it.
  5. Enter Resting before, Resistance (milliohms), Brownout and the charge state; save.

The battery's card shows your run, its resting voltage and a Ready or Check pill.

More detail
  • The Beak reads internal resistance, and it also shows Good, Fair or Bad.
  • These are WPILib's numbers: over 0.020 ohm means consider pulling it from matches; resting at 12.5 V means swap it. Full charge reads 12.7 to 13.5 V.
  • What was it: for example Bench test.
  • Resting before is in volts. Resistance is under More: resistance, minutes, event, notes.
  • Set Brownout to Yes if the robot browned out on it: that battery needs an analyzer test before anyone trusts it again.
  • Tap the charge state that fits: Charged, Charging, Needs charge or In robot.
That is not what I see
My battery shows CheckThe app flags a brownout in the last 3 runs, a resting voltage under 12.5 V before a match, or resistance over 20 milliohms. Fix the cause or keep it for practice.
The Beak says Good but resistance climbs every weekAndyMark says to watch the trend, not one reading. Note it, and test it on the analyzer.
The reading jumps between triesMeasure at least twice and record the steady value.

Module 2.5

Choose motors and controllers for 2027

40 min

Your motor list names each motor, its controller and its breaker, and totals 18 or fewer.

More detail

Module 3.1

Get the robot inspection-ready

45 min

You will: Run the electrical half of an inspection on the robot and fix what fails.

  1. Test isolation, battery unplugged: each SB50 post to frame must read over 120 ohms.
  2. Check the main breaker is quick to reach from outside, not behind panels or parts.
  3. Check the PD, wiring, breakers and radio lights are visible, and the RSL from 36 in away.
  4. Check seated breakers, covered terminals, one wire per PD terminal, a secured battery.
  5. Write a recheck list with an owner: all after match 1 or 2, bolts every 1 to 3 matches.

Your checklist is filled in for the real robot, with a pass or a fix on every line.

More detail
  • Isolation: set a meter to resistance and measure from each post of the robot's SB50 to bare frame. Over 120 ohms on both posts passes.
  • The main breaker must not sit behind panels or near moving parts. The RSL must be visible from 36 in away on at least one side.
  • A swerve drive uses 8 of the 18, which leaves 10 for mechanisms. Count motors (18 max, 2027 preview), and confirm nothing heats or cools the battery or breakers (new 2027 preview rule).
  • Recheck everything after the first match or two, and bolted connections every 1 to 3 matches.
  • No official 2027 inspection checklist exists yet; it comes with the Jan 9, 2027 manual. Rebuild your list from it then.
That is not what I see
Isolation reads under 120 ohmsSomething conducts to the frame. Look for pinched insulation or bare metal touching frame, fix it, then measure again.
The RSL is hidden behind a plateMove it: it must be visible from 36 in away on at least one side.
Which rules are final?None for 2027 until Jan 9, 2027. Use the 2026 rules as the baseline.

Module 3.2

Troubleshoot with lights and logs

45 min

You will: Find why a robot stopped or stuttered using status lights, the SystemCore dashboard and match logs.

  1. Start at the lights: WPILib Status Light Quick Reference for radio, PD and controllers.
  2. On SystemCore, join the robot's network and open its web dashboard at robot.local.
  3. Review the driver station log after every match; AdvantageScope opens WPILog files.
  4. Look for voltage dips: a sharp sag right when the robot fails points at power, not code.
  5. Check in order: battery (Beak and app log), main bolts and crimps, CAN plugs, then code.

You can name the failing part and the evidence (a light, log line or reading) behind it.

More detail
  • The Status Light Quick Reference has no SystemCore entry yet. The RSL flashes when enabled and stays solid when disabled.
  • The 2027 FIRST Driver Station writes WPILog files.
  • A sag points at the battery or main power.
  • Main power means its bolts and crimps.
  • Log a battery that browned out in the app with Brownout set to Yes.

Module 3.3

Build the SystemCore bench board

90 min

You will: Wire a bench board that powers SystemCore, the radio and one motor, for programmers to test on.

  1. Plan it first on paper or in draw.io: every part from the battery to one motor.
  2. Power SystemCore from battery voltage via its Micro-Fit port, on its own 10 A PD branch.
  3. Give the radio its own 10 A branch, and wire the RSL to SystemCore's RSL port.
  4. Wire one motor controller on a 40 A breaker with 12 AWG, and CAN from SystemCore.
  5. Power up, join SYSTEMCORE Wi-Fi, open robot.local, and Change Team Number to 5669.

robot.local shows team 5669, the RSL is solid (disabled), and the radio lights are on.

More detail
  • Parts: battery, SB50, 120 A main breaker, PD, SystemCore, radio, RSL, one motor controller and motor. The board lets programmers test before the robot exists.
  • Use the cable that ships with SystemCore. Its own 10 A branch is the 2026 rule for the controller. Never feed it from a regulator such as a VRM.
  • Run CAN from a SystemCore bus to the motor controller and on to the PD, terminated at the end.
  • Label every wire and its bus (for example can_s0), then pull-test and have a second person check every connection.
  • Plan around the hardware: SystemCore sales open Nov 18, 2026, and every team gets one in the Kickoff Kit. Until one arrives, finish the plan and parts list.

Module 3.4

  4. Pull-test every learner's work; a second person checks it before it goes on a robot.
  5. Give a subsystem to plan, wire and debug, with one planted fault (a loose CAN plug).

Your learner's crimps pass the pull test, and they find the planted fault without you pointing to it.
