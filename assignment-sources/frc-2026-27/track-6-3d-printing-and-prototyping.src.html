Module 1.1

Get cleared for the print lab

20 min

You will: Follow the print lab rules and know what must happen before you touch a printer.

  1. Read the attached Bambu Lab Users Guide, pages 2 and 13, before you print.
  2. Get the IDEA instructor's OK for every print; FRC prints also need the mech lead's.
  3. Inspect the printer first: wear, loose parts, filament knots or anything broken.
  4. Treat the nozzle as 300 C (572 F) and the bed as 120 C (248 F); hands out until cool.
  5. Find the printer certification on the IDEA Certification Portal: it is the proof.

You can name both people who must approve an FRC print and the two temperatures that can burn you.

More detail
  • Page 2 is Safety and Use in IDEA; page 13 is Maintenance and Cleaning.
  • The mech lead is the FRC mechanical team leader.
  • Keep the print lab clean and organized. No horseplay: a broken printer is expensive to fix.
  • This track prepares you for the certification; only the certification proves the skill.

Module 1.2

Learn Bambu Studio

90 min

You will: Import a model into Bambu Studio, slice it and read the preview before printing.

  1. Open Bambu Studio on a lab computer; the team slices for every Bambu printer with it.
  2. Do all 3 parts of the Bambu Studio Academy course: Basics, Intermediate, Advanced.
  3. Import a STEP or STL file into the Prepare tab and place it on the plate.
  4. Click Slice plate, then open Preview and scrub through the layers from the bottom up.
  5. Download your Academy Certificate of Completion.

The Preview shows every layer of your part with a print time and filament use, and you have a certificate PDF.

More detail
  • Keep Bambu Studio open beside the course as you work.
  • Read the 4 settings the lab guide calls key: layer height, print speed, infill density, and nozzle and bed temperature for the filament.
  • Turn on supports only where the part has overhangs, then slice again and compare the print time.

Module 1.4

Print your two-color name tag

60 min plus print time

You will: Take your own SolidWorks part from CAD to a finished two-color print.

  1. Open your first-name tag from the CAD track; keep the letters as their own body.
  2. Save it out of SolidWorks as STEP or STL with File > Save As.
  3. Import it and check 1.75 x 0.75 x 0.185 in (44.45 x 19.05 x 4.70 mm) in Bambu Studio.
  4. Set the base to one filament and the letters to a second filament loaded in the AMS.
  5. Click Slice plate, check Preview shows the color change at the letters, then Print plate.

A tag 1.75 in long with your name in a second color, crisp edges, and a flat first layer.

More detail
  • The tag is 0.75 x 1.75 x 0.185 in, from the SolidWorks CAD track.
  • Watch the first 2 layers. If they do not stick flat, stop and fix it now.
  • When the plate cools, remove the tag and clean the plate.
That is not what I see
The tag shows up tiny in Bambu Studio.It came in at the wrong units. Accept Bambu Studio's offer to convert the units, or re-save from SolidWorks with inches set, then check the size again.
The whole tag printed in one color.The letters were not set to a second filament. Make the letters their own body in SolidWorks, re-import, and assign the second filament before slicing.
The first layer is lifting at a corner.Stop the print. Clean the plate, run leveling, and start again. A lifted corner only gets worse.
The second color will not load.Check the AMS spool for knots and make sure the filament end is cut clean, then load again.

Module 2.1

Pick the right filament for a robot part

30 min

You will: Choose PLA+, PETG, nylon-CF or TPU for a robot part and explain why.

  1. Start with PLA+ or PETG; look up any filament in the material table before loading.
  2. Use PLA+ for mounts, spacers and pulleys with metal inserts; keep it out of heat.
  3. Use PETG for covers, clips and spacers, in an enclosure, never on the Cool Plate.
  4. Use nylon-CF (PA6-CF or PAHT-CF) under real load; dry it, use a hardened nozzle.
  5. Use TPU where parts flex or squish (wheels, bumper parts); dry it at 70 C for 8 hours.

For any part you can name the filament, the reason, and the one rule from the material table that applies.

More detail
  • For most FRC parts, PLA+ and PETG cover everything you need.
  • The Bambu material table lists the nozzle, drying and enclosure rules for each filament.
  • PLA softens near 60 C, so a robot in a parked car can warp.
  • PETG prints in an enclosed printer for layer strength.
  • Drying nylon-CF is the next module.
That is not what I see
The lab only has PLA loaded.PLA is fine for prototypes and low-load parts that stay cool. For a part that ships on the robot, ask the queue runner for PETG or nylon-CF.
PETG part snapped in a hit.PETG can fail brittle under sudden strain. Move to nylon-CF for parts that take impacts.
Which filament is in the AMS?Check the slot on the printer screen and the label on the spool. Never guess the material.
Check yourself
You need a soft roller sleeve that squishes around a game piece to grip it. Which filament fits?

Module 2.2

Dry and print nylon-CF

30 min plus 8 to 12 hr drying

You will: Prepare and print a carbon fiber nylon part without ruining the nozzle or the part.

  1. Confirm the printer has a hardened steel nozzle; carbon fiber wears out a normal one.
  2. Use a 0.6 mm nozzle for PA6-CF or PAHT-CF. Bambu rates it highly recommended.
  3. Dry the spool the day before at 80 C for 8 to 12 hours; store it with desiccant.
  4. Print in an enclosed printer (X1C or H2D): bed at 100 to 120 C, nozzle at 260 to 300 C.
  5. Never print at 250 C or above on a PTFE-lined hotend. That is unsafe.

Smooth, matte layers with no popping or bubbles, and a part that does not bend by hand.

More detail
  • Start the dryer the day before you need the part. Drying is a step, not an option.
That is not what I see
The filament pops and the surface is rough.It took on moisture. Stop, dry it at 80 C for 8 to 12 hours, and store it with desiccant.
I need the part in 2 hours and the nylon is wet.You cannot rush drying. Print a PETG test fit now and the nylon part after it dries.
The layers peel apart easily.Fillers like carbon fiber add stiffness but reduce layer adhesion. Check orientation (next module) and that the enclosure stayed closed.
Check yourself
A mentor hands you a spool of PETG-CF (carbon fiber PETG) for a battery mount. What does the material table require that plain PETG does not?

Module 2.3

Design a printed part to carry load

60 min

You will: Orient and shape a printed part so it survives the loads on a robot.

  1. Know the rule: a print is strong in 2 directions, weak in 1: layers pulling apart.
  2. Find the main load, then orient the part so that load runs along the layers.
  3. Split a part with no flat face on a plane in SolidWorks; screw the halves together.
  4. Avoid side loads on thin printed features. Let metal carry the load where it can.
  5. Use metal inserts where screws thread in; FRC teams do on mounts, spacers, pulleys.

In Preview, the layers run along the direction the part gets pulled or bent.

More detail
  • Do not chamfer the edge that touches the plate.
  • Raise infill density for strength, and add supports only under overhangs.
That is not what I see
My part broke cleanly between two layers.The load pulled the layers apart. Re-orient so the load runs along the layers, or split the part.
Orienting for strength needs a ton of supports.Split the part on a plane so each half sits flat, then screw them together.
Screws strip out of the plastic.Add metal inserts so the threads are in metal, not plastic.
Check yourself
A long, thin printed arm is fixed at one end and bends like a diving board when a game piece hits the other end. How should it sit on the plate?

Module 3.1

Run a rapid prototype test in build season

2 hr

You will: Build a fast prototype, test it one variable at a time and log what you learn.

  1. Start the Prototype Validation Log: quote the manual rule, name a past elite robot.
  2. Assign every role in the log; Spectator is not a role.
  3. Build the cheapest test of the idea; print small test parts, not whole mechanisms.
  4. Fill in the Prototype Test Run Sheet: 3 active variables max, the rest as constants.
  5. Run trials: pass or fail with a specific failure mode, then verdict and next change.

A filled test run sheet where each change in results can be traced to one changed variable.

More detail
  • Watch the 5 min Formlabs video for the general idea of rapid prototyping. It is an industry explainer, not FRC.
  • Quote the game manual rule the mechanism must meet. The elite robot is one from a past game that solved the same problem.
  • Roles: Project Lead, Lead Builder, CAD Librarian, Test Operator, Data Recorder, Runner.
  • Write every setting that is not an active variable down as a constant.
  • If you bend polycarbonate, add bend relief or it cracks (SendCutSend bending guidelines).
  • 2363 Triple Helix Robotics' drivetrain series is 13 videos; the Ri3D advice thread is from Mar 2026.
  • Log your build hours in the Techmen app.
That is not what I see
We changed 4 things and it finally worked.You are guessing, not engineering. Lock one variable as a constant and rerun so you know which change mattered.
The form shows 2026 dates.Those are from the 2026 season. Use the freeze date the team sets for 2027 after the Jan 9, 2027 kickoff.
We do not know where to start the design.Look at how other teams built it. The 2363 drivetrain series and Ri3D advice favor a KOP chassis and simple, easy-to-copy mechanisms.
Check yourself
Run 5 shot well. Run 6 changed only the shooter angle, with RPM, compression and wheel type held constant, and fell short every trial. What did you learn?

Module 3.2

Use the H2D laser and vinyl cutter safely

60 min

You will: Run the H2D laser and cutting modules safely, and cut vinyl bumper numbers.

  1. Read the H2D wiki Laser Module FAQ and Cutting Module FAQ before using either.
  2. Never defeat a safety system: the H2D is Class 1 only when all of them work.
  3. Laser only materials you can name; never PVC or polycarbonate (toxic fumes).
  4. Cut vinyl bumper numbers with the blade cutting module, never the laser. Vinyl is PVC.
  5. Run the ventilation Bambu requires every time the laser runs; stay with the machine.

Clean vinyl numbers cut with the blade, and a laser job run with the door closed, key in and ventilation on.

More detail
  • The laser module alone is Class 4. The H2D safety systems: the door, module detection, flame and temperature detection, and the safety key.
  • ABS and HDPE melt. If you do not know what a plastic is, do not cut it.
  • Know the limit of this training: the H2D laser is not the shop fiber laser. The fiber laser needs its own certification.
That is not what I see
The laser will not start.A safety system is doing its job: check the door is closed, the module is seated and the safety key is in. Never bypass it.
Someone wants to laser cut a clear sheet for a guard.Clear robot sheet is often polycarbonate. Do not laser it. Cut it on a shop tool you are certified on.
The vinyl numbers have torn corners.Recheck the cutting module setup in the Cutting Module FAQ and run a small test letter first.
Check yourself
A teammate wants to laser engrave the team number on an unlabeled scrap of clear plastic with the H2D. What do you do?

Module 3.3

Run the team print queue

Ongoing, 60 min to start

You will: Take print requests from the team, plan the printers' day and keep them running.

  1. Log every print request as a Techmen app job; none lives only in someone's head.
  2. Check each job: requester cleared, filament matches the part, file is the newest CAD.
  3. Start long steps first: dry nylon-CF 8 to 12 hr ahead, fill printers with ready jobs.
  4. Match job to printer: carbon fiber only on a hardened nozzle, PETG and nylon enclosed.
  5. Teach one newer member a week to run a job start to finish with the Level 1 modules.

The jobs list shows every request with its status, and the printers are rarely idle while ready jobs wait.

More detail
  • Clean the plate after every print. Check drive gears, lubricate the axes and tighten loose parts on a regular schedule.
  • Log your hours in the Techmen app.
That is not what I see
Everyone says their part is the most urgent.Robot parts for the next test or event come first, then spares, then everything else. Ask the mech lead to break ties.
A printer keeps failing first layers.Clean the plate, run leveling and check the nozzle. If it still fails, stop using it and report it in the Techmen app feedback.
A request came in with no filament named.Send it back to the designer. A part printed in the wrong filament is a wasted print.
Check yourself
Two jobs come in at the end of practice: a nylon-CF gearbox plate for tomorrow's test (spool not dried) and a PLA cable clip for next week. One printer is free. Best plan?
