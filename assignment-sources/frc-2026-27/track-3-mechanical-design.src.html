Done when: You can design a COTS-based FRC mechanism to the team's hardware standards, prove it with prototype data, detail its plates with GD&T, defend it in a design review and a judge interview, and coach a newer member through the same work.

Module 1.1

Read a robot and its size rules

60 min

You will: Name robot parts, use house hardware, check 2026 size, weight and bumper rules.

  1. Find the 5 part groups on the FIRST KitBot page and on one Behind the Bumpers robot.
  2. Learn the house standard: 2x1 and 1x1 tube, 0.5 in hole pitch, #10-32 screws, hex shaft.
  3. Check the 2026 rules (until Jan 9, 2027): 110.0 in perimeter, 30 in tall, 115.0 lb.
  4. Open the Techmen app Robot page; label a robot photo: 5 groups, perimeter.

Your labeled photo shows frame, bumpers, battery, electronics and each mechanism, perimeter traced on the frame.

More detail
  • The 5 part groups: frame and drivetrain, bumpers, battery, electronics on the bellypan, and each mechanism.
  • Full house standard, shared by WCP, REV and Thrifty: 2x1 and 1x1 aluminum tube in 1/16 or 1/8 in wall, holes on a 0.5 in pitch, #10-32 screws, 3/16 in rivets, 1/2 in and 3/8 in rounded hex shaft.
  • Size (2026 rules until the Jan 9, 2027 kickoff): perimeter at most 110.0 in and starting height at most 30 in (R104); at most 12 in out past the perimeter (R105), in one direction at a time (R106). Minor protrusions up to 0.25 in do not count toward perimeter (R101).
  • Weight and cost (2026): 115.0 lb without bumpers and battery (R103), 135.0 lb with bumpers (R408), $600 maximum for any single item (R301).
  • Bumpers (2026): zone 2.5 to 5.75 in off the floor (R405); padding at least 2.25 in deep and 4.5 in tall on backing at least 4.5 in tall (R402); fastened rigidly with no hook-and-loop or zip ties, at most 4.0 in out from the perimeter (R403); corners filled at least 2.25 in (R406); no wedge shapes (R407).
  • The one confirmed 2027 change: at most 18 motors on the robot. The 2027 game is BIOCORE; its own size numbers arrive with the manual on Jan 9, 2027.

Module 1.2

Design with FRC fasteners

2 sessions

You will: Pick the screw, nut, rivet and threadlocker for a joint, size its hole, and show it on a drawing.

  1. Read the FRC sizes and the FRC Fasteners nut section: Keps, Nyloc and PEM nuts.
  2. In SolidWorks, model a 6.0 x 4.0 x 0.125 in plate with clearance, tapped and rivet holes.
  3. Draw it: Front view, overall size, holes located from edges, Hole Callout on each.
  4. Assemble: fix the plate at the origin; Concentric and Coincident mate hardware.
  5. Save the .SLDPRT and .SLDDRW, then Pack and Go as YourName_FastenerAssembly.ZIP.

Your drawing has three labeled hole rows with a callout on every hole; each assembly screw sits flush.

More detail
  • Sizes: #10-32 is the main size, 1/4-20 for high-strength joints, #8-32 and #6-32 for small parts, #4-40 for light covers and sensors.
  • Clearance holes: 4-40 at 0.125, 6-32 at 0.156, 8-32 at 0.172, #10 at 0.203, 1/4-20 at 0.266 in.
  • Tapped holes: 4-40 #43 drill (0.0890), 6-32 #36 (0.1065), 8-32 #29 (0.1360), 10-32 at 0.159 in (the WCP hex through-hole size), 1/4-20 #7 (0.2010).
  • Rivet holes: 1/8 in rivet #30 drill, 5/32 in #20, 3/16 in #11. Rule: every riveted structural joint also gets at least 1 bolt.
  • Drawing: locate every hole from the plate edges. The 8-32 clearance callout reads Ø0.172 THRU.
  • McMaster-Carr hardware to add: 8-32 screw with a Keps nut, #10-32 screw with a standard nut, 1/4-20 screw with a Nyloc nut, and a 3/16 in rivet.
  • Name the part and drawing YourName_TestPlate.SLDPRT and YourName_TestPlate.SLDDRW. Pack and Go zips the assembly.

Module 1.3

Write an engineering notebook entry

45 min

You will: Write one complete entry, with numbers instead of opinions, that someone else could continue from.

  1. Know the rule: FRC has no required notebook; judges hear your process in the pit.
  2. Pick a task you did yourself this week: CAD change, prototype test, assembly or math.
  3. Write the header: Title and version, Date, Author(s), a 1 to 2 sentence Objective.
  4. Write Process and Methodology in time order: photos, sketches, math, what you saw.
  5. Write Results with numbers, then Conclusions and Next Steps that your results defend.

Your entry has all six parts, at least one number in Results, and a next step that follows from that number.

More detail
  • Judges learn your process by interviewing you in the pit. Excellence in Engineering is judged mainly on process. Your entries are where that process story comes from.
  • Any format works: doc, slides or paper. The Title says what and which version; the Objective names the question you were answering.
  • Caption every photo or sketch, and include every calculation.
  • Results: trials, success count, percent, times. Strong example: 8 of 10 trials (80%), average 1.2 s, versus 50% for V1.
  • Conclusions and Next Steps: say whether the objective was met, defend the next step with your results, and name it.
  • Last test: if I am not here tomorrow, could someone continue from this alone? Then log your hours in the Techmen app.

I do not have any numbers.Count something: trials and successes, seconds per cycle, a measured gap in inches. Then write it down.
I only did CAD today.Post a screenshot, describe what changed and why, and list the next step.

Module 2.1

Integrate COTS parts and space gears

2 sessions

You will: Lay out a two-stage gearbox plate with correct center distances; prove it turns.

  1. Make one project folder for this job, save every file in it, and get vendor STEP files.
  2. Model Gearbox_Plate: 4 x 6 in, 0.25 in thick, dimensions in shop fractions (0.125, 0.375).
  3. Compute C-C = (N1 + N2) / (2 x DP) at 20 DP, plus 0.002 in: 1.802 in and 1.852 in.
  4. Assemble: fixed plate, motor, bearings, 1/2 in hex shafts, gears, a Gear mate per mesh.
  5. Drag the pinion: the output turns about 10.4:1 slower. Screenshot it, then Pack and Go.

Dragging the pinion turns the whole train, and the output shaft turns about 10 times slower than the motor.

More detail
  • Download STEP files from vendor product pages: WCP, REV, AndyMark, SDS, The Thrifty Bot, and McMaster-Carr for hardware.
  • Keep dimensions to fractions of an inch so the shop can make the plate.
  • 12T to 60T: 72 / 40 = 1.800 in. 24T to 50T: 74 / 40 = 1.850 in. The 0.002 in center-add keeps gears from binding. Confirm in the ReCalc Gears calculator.
  • Copy the motor hole pattern from the motor's STEP file (Kraken X60 or NEO). Both mount with #10-32.
  • Do the assembly in SolidWorks. Mate the motor with Concentric and Coincident. The 24T sits on the 60T's shaft as a cluster gear. The Gear mate is under Mechanical Mates and uses tooth counts.
  • Ratio: 60/12 x 50/24, about 10.4:1. Take screenshots from angles that show every part.
That is not what I see
The guide asks for a CIM, VersaPlanetary or VersaWheel from VEXpro.VEXpro has effectively left FRC and those parts are sold out or delisted. Use a Kraken X60 or NEO, WCP PlanetaryX or REV MAXPlanetary, and a current wheel.
Parts fly apart or vanish when I reopen the assembly.Files were saved outside the project folder. Move every part into one folder and reopen.
I need a belt or chain center distance, not gears.Use the ReCalc Belt or Chain calculator, or the AMB calculator's C-C tool. Do not guess an extra-distance number.
Spectrum's slides show different motors.Spectrum Design Concepts is from 2017. Its physics still holds; its parts list is CIM-era.

Module 2.2

Design a robust shaft stackup

2 sessions

You will: Lay out a live axle between plates that spins freely, stays put lengthwise and can be made in the shop.

  1. Set up two 1/8 in aluminum plates 2.500 in apart, a 1/2 in ThunderHex shaft, a gear.
  2. Choose spacers with bolts through, or threaded standoffs (#8 or #10 bolts); write why.
  3. Size each spacer: (gap minus part width) / 2. A 0.500 in gear needs 1.000 in each side.
  4. Retain each bearing (for example under bolt heads) and model retaining ring grooves.
  5. Draw the shaft: each hex, ThunderHex and round section and groove size; justify it.

The shaft spins, every part is fully constrained, and the drawing gives every section and groove size.

More detail
  • Setup: a plate and standoff build, a round radial ball bearing in each plate, one COTS gear or pulley centered between them.
  • Spacers: steel bolts pass through them, so the bolt is in tension and preloads the joint. Standoffs: threaded, bolted from each end.
  • The spacer example assumes bearings flush to the inside faces. Each bearing must not be able to fall out of its plate.
  • Grooves go next to the gear and near the outer end on one side. Turn the shaft down to round next to each groove so rings slide on easily.
  • Add 0.01 in to the length of hex or ThunderHex sections for part-width variation. Laser or waterjet slots and tabs: make slots 0.005 in bigger or tabs 0.005 in smaller.
  • Groove widths and diameters come from McMaster-Carr values. Write a short justification.
That is not what I see
Live axle or dead axle?Live: the shaft turns and carries torque, easier from a gearbox. Dead: the shaft stays still and the part spins on it, and the shaft can stiffen the frame.
The retaining ring will not slide over the hex.Turn the shaft down to round next to the groove, as the guide says.
My spacers add up longer than the gap.Subtract the part width first, then split what is left between the two sides.
Check yourself
Two bearings sit flush in plates 3.000 in apart. A 0.750 in wide pulley must be centered. How long is each shaft spacer?

Module 3.1

Draw a gearbox plate with GD&T

2 sessions

You will: Make a drawing that controls what a gearbox depends on, so any plate made to it meshes the same way.

  1. Know why: plain dimensions miss how features relate; small C-C error causes backlash.
  2. Model a 1/4 in aluminum plate: 12:60 then 24:50 at 20 DP, 1/2 in hex bearing bores.
  3. On an ANSI B-size sheet, place Front, Top, Right and Isometric views.
  4. Dimension it, then Datum Feature: A large flat face, B long bottom edge, C short edge.
  5. Add Geometric Tolerance to A, B, C; export a PDF; check it against 254's tutorial.

Your PDF shows three datums, a profile callout on the outline, and one position callout on all bearing bores.

More detail
  • Plain dimensions with a title block tolerance do not control how features relate. A plain diameter tolerance does not stop an oval or tapered bearing bore.
  • The plate is a two-stage reduction. Copy the motor holes from your motor's STEP file, and add a perimeter hole pattern for the chassis and standoffs.
  • Use a full title block (Drivetrain Gearbox Plate, material, designer, date). The Isometric view goes top right.
  • Dimension the overall size, every motor hole, every bearing bore, and the perimeter hole pattern. Datum C is the short edge adjacent to B.
  • Callouts: Profile of a Surface on the outer boundary; one Position tolerance on the whole bearing bore pattern; Position on the motor hole pattern. All reference A, B, C.
  • 254's gearbox plate tutorial is on 254 Resources.
That is not what I see
The guide is built around three CIM motors.The guide dates from the CIM era. Keep the same gear stages and controls, and swap in the hole pattern of a current motor from its STEP file.
I do not know what the symbols mean.Look them up on the GD&T Basics symbol page. The current standard is ASME Y14.5-2018.
Why one Position callout instead of dimensions to each bore?Separate dimensions let errors stack and change the distance between bores. One pattern callout keeps that spacing fixed.

Module 3.2

Run a design review and build the tech binder

2 sessions

You will: Lead a subsystem design review, turn it into a judge-ready binder page, and explain it problem to part.

  1. Read the Award Workbook criteria and the 2027 Judges Tip Sheet: process is judged.
  2. Gather a subsystem's entries: problem, options, design, calculations, tests, failures.
  3. Review it before anything is cut: rules, house hardware, and pit repair speed.
  4. Build a 254-style binder page: a large render, a one-sentence job, key bullets, fixes.

One binder page with a render, a one-sentence job and key bullets, plus review actions with an owner each.

More detail

  • Add the issues you hit and how you fixed them. Write it so judges, scouts and kids all follow it. Organize the binder by subsystem; keep business material in a separate binder.
  • Interview: trace the subsystem from concept to completion in a few short sentences, then answer one open question. Judges keep interviews short and end with an open question.
  • Have the Level 1 member write their own entry, and review it with them against the six-part structure.

Module 3.3

Take a prototype to final design

3 sessions

You will: Lead a prototype from rule to locked design, with CAD matching what was proven.

  1. Start the Prototype Validation Log: the rule it serves, a past elite robot, motor, gearing.
  2. Assign the 6 roles in the log; spectator is not a role.
  3. Sketch many concepts fast; check packaging and collisions before cutting anything.
  4. Test on the Prototype Test Run Sheet: 3 active variables max, up to 15 trials per run.
  5. Lock it: caliper the rig, update CAD, re-check limits, then mark GREENLIGHT or KILL.

Your validation log has 4 signed gates, attached run sheets, a success rate, and CAD matching as-built.

More detail
  • Log: quote the game manual rule this mechanism serves, name an elite robot from a past game that solved the same physics, and write the starting motor, gearing and estimated output.
  • Spectator is not a role. Roles: Project Lead, Lead Builder, CAD Librarian, Test Operator, Data Recorder, Runner.
  • Concepts: napkin sketches, cardboard rigs. Packaging check: inside frame and height limits. Collision check: against bumpers and floor.
  • Each run: everything else written down as a constant, pass or fail, and a specific failure mode for each trial.
  • Pick the best configuration by its success rate against the team's goal (for example 90%), or score the options with a weighted objective table.
  • Lock it with the best rig: measure it with calipers and update the CAD to those real values. A mentor signs each of the 4 gates.
  • Then move from blocky Crayola-CAD to detailed design, and post the open tasks for the team on the Techmen app Jobs page.
