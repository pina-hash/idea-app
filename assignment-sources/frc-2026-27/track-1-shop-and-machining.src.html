Module 1.1

Read the shop safety rules and gear up

90 min

You will: Name the PPE for each machine, spot its 3 danger zones, and pass the safety test.

  1. Read Harvard EHS Machine Shops (8 sections) and the OSHA Machine Guarding eTool.
  2. Read the FIRST Safety Manual sections Participant Responsibilities and PPE.
  3. Gear up: rated glasses, closed shoes, hair tied back. Gloves off at spinning tools.
  4. Pass the safety test on the IDEA Certification Portal during FRC hours: 80% or higher.

Your shop safety certification shows as passed on ideabosco.com/certifications.

More detail
  • Harvard sections: rules poster, machine guarding terms, band saw, cutoff saws, drill press, general shop safety, lathe, milling machine.
  • OSHA's 3 hazard areas: point of operation, power transmission, operating controls.
  • Safety glasses must be rated, with no reflective lenses. Shoes are closed toe and heel. Secure loose clothing and long hair. Wear hearing protection for loud tools.
  • Take gloves off at the band saw, drill press, mill and lathe. Spinning tools grab gloves.
  • Read the team rules in the Techmen app Handbook and find the Emergency info page.

Module 1.3

Cut and drill stock safely

60 min

You will: Cut stock to rough length on the band saw and drill a located hole on the drill press.

  1. Mark the cut line a little long with a tape measure or ruler. You will face it to size later.
  2. Cut on the horizontal or vertical band saw (recommended). Gloves off.
  3. Mark hole centers with the height gauge (precise and steady) or calipers.
  4. Center drill on the tabletop drill press, then drill to size on the standing press.
  5. Deburr every edge and hole, then clean up chips with a brush, never your hand.

A square-ish cut a little over length and clean round holes right on your marks, no sharp edges.

More detail
  • Avoid the cutoff saw and angle grinder for this: loud, and they spray hot metal.
  • Center drill each hole first so the full-size drill does not wander.
  • On the standing drill press, hold the part in a vise.
  • Pick the drill for each hole from the Drill Size Chart.

Module 1.4

Make your first machined part

2 sessions

You will: Turn a drawing into a finished, inspected aluminum tube part within +/-0.05 in.

  1. Model the part and drawing in the SolidWorks CAD track first, then print the drawing.
  2. Gear up: safety glasses, smock, hearing protection if loud. No gloves at spinning machines.
  3. Band saw the 6061-T6 tube a little over 3.00 in, then face both ends square on the mill.
  4. Mark 3 holes with the height gauge, center drill, then drill per the Drill Size Chart.
  5. Deburr every edge, stamp your initials, photograph it on your drawing, and log hours.

A 3.00 in tube with 3 clean holes on location, square ends, no sharp edges, initials top right.

More detail
  • Keep the printed drawing with you in the shop.
  • Nitrile gloves are only for handling stock. Take them off at every spinning machine.
  • Clamp the tube lightly in the vise: hollow tube crushes.
  • Stamp your initials top right as drawn, then clean your mess.
  • Photograph the part lying on your printed drawing in good light.
  • Log the session in the Techmen app Log hours.

Module 2.1

Calculate speeds and feeds

45 min

You will: Work out spindle RPM and feed rate for a cutter, material and machine.

  1. Find your material's SFM in Speeds and Feeds: aluminum 600 to 1000, mild steel 100 to 300.
  2. Mill RPM = (SFM x 3.82) / cutter diameter in inches.
  3. Feed rate (IPM) = RPM x number of flutes x chip load per tooth.
  4. Lathe RPM = (cutting speed m/min x 1000) / (3.14 x part diameter mm).
  5. Start light on a new tool or material, then adjust by sound and chip.

You can write RPM and IPM for a cut before you touch the machine.

More detail
  • The SFM ranges above are for uncoated tools.
  • Example: 1/2 in uncoated end mill in mild steel at 100 SFM = 764 RPM. 2 flutes at 0.002 in/tooth = about 3.1 IPM.
  • Coated tools (TiN gold, TiCN blue-gray) can run 1.25x to 2x faster than uncoated HSS.
  • The lathe formula is metric, from the Lathe Manual. Cutting speed: aluminum 150 to 200 m/min, mild steel 80 to 150 m/min.
That is not what I see
My RPM is higher than the machine can go.Run at the top speed you have and calculate feed from that RPM, not the ideal one.
I cannot tell if the end mill is coated.Gold is TiN, blue-gray is TiCN, silver-gray is CrN. Plain steel color is uncoated HSS.
Lathe formula uses mm and my drawing is in inches.Multiply inches by 25.4 to get mm first.

Set up the lathe and turn Lathe Parts 1 and 2

2 sessions

  1. Get permission from Mr. Pina and the mechanical lead every time. Short sleeves only.
  2. Center the stock in the 3-jaw chuck with the T handle, tighten gradually, remove it.
  3. Lock the tool in the quick-change tool post at center height. Hand-turn to check.

Each diameter measures within 0.010 in of the drawing and the surface is smooth with no jagged cuts.

More detail

Module 2.3

Thread a bolt and design Lathe Part 4

2 sessions

You will: Cut a single-point external thread and plan a part with 4 lathe features.

  1. Lathe Part 3: turn a 3/8 in bolt with a 10 TPI thread (sizes under More detail).
  2. Set the threading knobs and gears to 10 TPI from the lathe's threading chart. Slow the spindle.
  3. Engage the lead screw, take light passes, and retract the tool at the end of every pass.
  4. Lathe Part 4: plan a 3 in part on 1 in aluminum bar with any 4 lathe features.
  5. Face both ends (required, not a feature), sketch Part 4 with dimensions, make it, log hours.

A bolt whose thread matches 10 TPI on a gauge, and a 3 in aluminum part with 4 different features.

More detail
  • Lathe Part 3 bolt: 3/8 in diameter, 1-1/2 in thread length, 1/4 in head, 9/16 in across the hex flats.
  • 10 TPI is a practice pitch, so the finished bolt will not take a standard 3/8-16 nut.
  • Add depth a little each pass. Check the thread with a thread gauge or calipers before the last pass.
  • Part 4 features to pick from: partial internal bore, internal thread, external thread, contour, chamfer, knurl, drill.

Set up the mill and make Mill Parts 1 and 2

2 sessions

You will: Change tooling, move to exact positions with the dials, and mill and tap a block.

  1. Read the Mill Presentation and Mill Guide safety pages. No gloves or jewelry.
  2. Swap tools: loosen the draw bar 2 to 3 turns, tap its top, swap, then retighten firmly.
  3. Read the dials: each dash 0.001 in, 200 dashes per turn, 5 turns = 1 in.
  4. Make Mill Part 1 (tapped block) and Mill Part 2 (step plate); sizes under More detail.

A block at 1.000 in on every side within 0.010 in with a clean 1/2-20 thread, and a step plate to drawing.

More detail
  • At the mill: no gloves, jewelry or loose sleeves.
  • Tap the top of the draw bar with a soft-faced hammer to free the tool.
  • Take out backlash: always approach your number turning the same direction.
  • Mill Part 1: square a 1 x 1 x 1 in aluminum block with a 1/2-20 tapped hole through the center.
  • For the 1/2-20 hole, drill the tap drill size from the chart, not 0.500 in.
  • Tap by hand: tap wrench, tapping oil, tap parallel to the hole, a few turns then back off to clear chips.
  • Mill Part 2: the step plate, 4 x 2 x 1/2 in with two steps on each end (see drawing).
  • Brush chips off with the machine off.
  • This Old Tony is a manual machining channel.

Module 3.2

Bend sheet metal that does not crack

2 sessions

You will: Pick a bendable material, keep features off the bend, and bend a bracket.

  1. Pick the material: mild steel or 5052-H32 aluminum. 6061 cracks when bent: use it flat.
  2. Keep holes and cutouts at least half the die width from the bend centerline.
  3. Use a K-factor of about 0.4 for aluminum. Add bend relief on polycarbonate.
  4. Cut blanks on the fiber laser or foot shear. Hands out of the shear's point of operation.
  5. Bend a scrap test piece on the brake, measure the angle, then bend the real part.

A bracket with a clean bend, no cracks on the outside of the bend, and holes that stayed round.

More detail
  • Bend the bracket on the sheet metal brake.
  • The team mostly bends mild steel. 5052-H32 aluminum also bends well. 6061 aluminum cracks at the bend.
  • Set the K-factor in your SolidWorks flat pattern.
  • Polycarbonate cracks at the bend without bend relief.
  • Fiber laser: 2 mm nozzle normally, ceramic nozzle for thick steel.
  • When laser welding runs, everyone else clears the shop.
That is not what I see
My aluminum cracked along the bend.It is almost surely 6061. Remake it in 5052-H32 or mild steel.
A hole next to the bend stretched into an oval.Move features at least half the die width from the bend centerline.
I want to cut polycarbonate on the H2D laser.Never. Polycarbonate burns and releases toxic fumes. Never laser PVC either.
I did H2D laser training. Can I run the fiber laser?No. The fiber laser is a different, more dangerous laser with its own certification.

  2. Walk the shop with the Appendix A checklist. Every no gets an Appendix B fix.

A trainee's passing part, a filled training record, a finished checklist with action items, a posted SOP.

More detail

  • You run the safety walk as safety captain.
  • Appendix A is the Safety Checklist. Appendix B is the Corrective and Preventative Action Plan.
  • Record the training on the Harvard training record form, then both of you log hours.
