Module 1.2

Pass the built-in tutorials by mass

3 hr

You will: Follow SolidWorks' own tutorials and prove each part is right with Mass Properties.

  1. Click the Help (?) icon at the top right, then Tutorials. No internet needed.
  2. Do Introduction to SolidWorks, then Basic Techniques (list under More detail).
  3. Check each part: Evaluate tab > Mass Properties, against the mass key.
  4. Name each file with your name and tutorial, save it to the vault, and log hours.

Mass Properties shows the key's mass to two decimals, in grams.

More detail
  • Basic Techniques: Revolves and Sweeps, Fillets, Pattern Features, Lofts.
  • Mass key: Introduction 152.89 g; Revolves and Sweeps 1109.57 g; Fillets 10.02 g and 9.18 g; Pattern Features 47.52 g; Lofts has no mass check.
  • Then do Assembly Mates, 3D Sketching, 3D Sketching with Planes, Multibody Parts, Mouse Gestures and Animation.
  • Name files like "name - revolves and sweeps".
  • Save every file to the team file vault.

Module 1.3

Model parts from dimensioned images

2 hr

You will: Read a dimensioned picture and model it with no tutorial to follow.

  1. Open Practice Part 1 to 5 and set the part units to MMGS before you sketch.
  2. Model each from its base shape, dimension every sketch, and fix the sketch, not the 3D.
  3. Then design a two-color nametag in IPS, save it as a part and STEP, and 3D print it.

Each part matches every dimension in its image, and the nametag prints in two colors.

More detail
  • Practice Part numbers are millimeters. Set MMGS in the status bar at the bottom right.
  • Find the base shape first: the biggest feature you can sketch on one plane.
  • Build with Extruded Boss/Base, Revolved Boss/Base, Extruded Cut, Fillet and Mirror.
  • Compare each feature to the image. Fix the sketch, not the 3D result.
  • Nametag: 0.75 x 1.75 x 0.185 in, your first name, two colors (background and name).
  • Print the nametag under the Print track rules.

Module 2.1

Design and draw your first machined part

2 hr

You will: Model a real shop part and make a drawing a machinist can build from.

  1. Play the 1st Machined Part walkthrough with SolidWorks open.
  2. Model it in IPS from the drawing: a 2.00 x 3.00 in box section in 6061-T6.
  3. Size the holes from the drawing and the Drill Size Chart: 0.26 in is a letter G drill.
  4. Click File > Make Drawing from Part/Assembly, place 3 views, and dimension them.
  5. Fill the title block, save a PDF, turn in part and PDF, then make it in the Shop track.

One sheet with three views, every hole located from an edge, and a full title block, like the reference.

More detail
  • Model from 1st Machined Part drawing.pdf.
  • Place the Front, Top and Right views.
  • On the Annotation tab, use Smart Dimension for sizes and Hole Callout for holes.
  • Title block: your name, 6061-T6, inches, ±0.05 tolerance, DEBURR ALL EDGES.

Module 2.2

Assemble a gearbox with mates

3 hr

You will: Build an assembly where parts move like the real mechanism.

  1. Do the built-in Assembly Mates tutorial first (Basic Techniques).
  2. Model the 4 x 6 x 0.25 in gearbox plate from COTS Component Integration.pdf.
  3. Start a new assembly and insert the plate first; the first part is fixed in place.
  4. Click Mate: add Concentric and Coincident for each bearing, shaft and gear.
  5. Add a Gear mate per gear pair, then drag the motor pinion: the wheel turns slowest.

Dragging one gear turns every gear, shaft and the wheel together at their ratios.

More detail
  • Gear mates are under Mechanical Mates, typed by tooth count.
  • Dragging the pinion should turn the whole train.
  • Save as Drivetrain_Assembly.SLDASM in the same project folder as every part.
That is not what I see
A part will not move at allIt may be fixed. Right-click it and choose Float, unless it is the base plate.
A mate flips the part the wrong wayIn the mate box, switch the alignment between Aligned and Anti-Aligned.
Gears pass through each otherGear mates do not stop overlap. Check the plate's hole spacing against the gear math.

Module 3.1

Lay out a mechanism top-down

4 hr

You will: Drive a whole mechanism from one layout sketch, the FRC way.

  1. Watch 973 RAMP "Designing Robots with Sketches": the method holds, parts are old.
  2. Draw a layout sketch first: frame lines, shaft centers and motor spots. No solids yet.
  3. Space gear pairs by C-C = (N1 + N2) / (2 x DP) + 0.002 in, and check it in ReCalc.
  4. In the assembly, build each plate from the layout with Convert Entities on its lines.
  5. Change one layout dimension and click Rebuild: every plate should follow.

Editing one layout dimension moves the shafts and resizes the plates with no broken features.

More detail
  • The RAMP videos date from about 2011 to 2013.
  • The 0.002 in is a center-add on top of the gear math.
  • Use configurations for parts that come in sizes (one shaft file, many lengths), as in RAMP Configurations Part 1 and 2.
  • Use FRCDesign.org's stage order as your map: fundamentals, power transmission, practice mechanisms, top-down, subsystems.
  • Spectrum Design Concepts (2017) has good fundamentals with CIM-era parts.
That is not what I see
A plate does not update after a layout changeClick Rebuild. If it still will not move, that plate was sketched by hand, not converted from the layout.
FRCDesign shows Onshape buttonsUse its order and ideas only: a Part Studio maps to a multibody part, a master sketch to a layout sketch, Onshape configurations to configurations or design tables.
The RAMP video uses CIM motorsThose parts are dated. Copy the sketch method, not the part choices.
Check yourself
Two 20 DP gears mesh: 18 teeth and 54 teeth. With a 0.002 in center-add, how far apart are the shaft centers?

Module 3.2

Control a drawing with GD&T

3 hr

You will: Add datums and Position tolerances so bearing holes and gears line up.

  1. Read GD&T Technical Drawing.pdf and model its 0.25 in gearbox plate.
  2. Make an ANSI B-size drawing: Front, Top, Right and Isometric views, full title block.
  3. Mark datums with Datum Feature: the big face A, a long edge B, a short edge C.
  4. Add a Geometric Tolerance Position callout to the bearing bores, referenced A, B, C.
  5. Check every symbol in the GD&T Basics library, then turn in the part, drawing and PDF.

Every critical hole carries a feature control frame that points to datums A, B and C.

More detail
  • The holes must line up when the part is made: that is what the tolerances control.
  • Add Position to the motor mount holes and Profile of a Surface to the outline.
  • The current standard is ASME Y14.5-2018.
  • Turn in the .SLDPRT, the .SLDDRW and a PDF of the drawing.
That is not what I see
I cannot find the GD&T toolsThey are on the Annotation tab: Datum Feature and Geometric Tolerance.
My callout has no datum lettersIn the Geometric Tolerance box, type A, B and C into the Primary, Secondary and Tertiary fields.
A source I found uses Y14.5-2009Y14.5-2018 replaced 2009. Follow 2018.
Check yourself
Four motor holes are located hole to hole with chained ±0.01 in dimensions. Worst case, how far off can hole 4 be from hole 1?

Module 3.4

Teach a new member their first part

1 hr per trainee

You will: Coach a beginner through a part hands-off, and check their work by evidence.

  1. Pick a task with its own answer key: a tutorial with a mass check, or the Quick Start final.
  2. Sit beside them. They drive; your hands stay off the mouse.
  3. When they are stuck, ask what the feature tree shows before you give any fix.
  4. Have them check their own part with Mass Properties against the key.
  5. Give less help each round, then point them to the next module and both log hours.

Your trainee finishes a part that passes its mass check, and you never took the mouse.

More detail
  • Give less help each round, the way FRCDesign.org's course gets less guided as it goes.
  • Log the hours in the Techmen app.
  • The Quick Start final needs 80% to pass.
  • The IDEA Certification Portal is the official proof of a skill. Training prepares for it, never replaces it.
