The rules · 5 min

The multi-part hook

  • Comes apart, rope in, closes again: by hand, in 10 s max. Printed keys only.
  • Both loops may be closed, Ø 1.000 in min. No opening rule.
  • Same centers and thickness. 0.11 lb (50.0 g) max for all pieces together.
  • Why: a standard hook pulls open at its open tip. A closed ring does not.
  • Why not: every joint is a new place to break. The record, 436 lb, is multi-part, but most of you do better with a standard hook.

Research · 10 min

Pick a 3D feature

One sketch, one extrude: a cookie-cutter hook. It works, but it wastes the thickness. The strong hooks are shaped in all three directions. Orange marks the feature.

  • Raised rim: the bowl bends, and bending stress is highest at its edges. A rim puts material there.
  • Thick bowl: the shank only pulls straight; the bowl bends. Give the thickness to the part that bends.
  • Bosses at the holes: more thickness where the pin and rope press.
  • Rounded edges: a sharp inside corner is where a crack starts. A fillet costs almost no mass.
Overhangs, supports and layers
  • Overhangs: a surface leaning past about 45 degrees prints over air and needs support under it.
  • Supports cost time and plastic and leave a rough face. Use them where the strength is worth it; reshape where it is not.
  • Layers: a print is weakest between its layers. Pulled along them it holds; pulled across them it splits.
