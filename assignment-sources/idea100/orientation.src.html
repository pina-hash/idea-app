Meet IDEA

IDEA is engineering design and manufacturing. It stands for Integrated Design, Engineering and Art. Every project goes through the same four stages:

  • Conceive: a need or a problem, a rough design, sketched by hand.
  • Design: refined in SolidWorks, the industry standard.
  • Manufacture: in the shop. 3D printing, mill, lathe, CNC, laser, sheet metal.
  • Test: a real trial. If it fails, back to the drawing board.

The art is still here: you sketch by hand, and you design things that look right.

During robot season, the IDEA classes and the Techmen robotics team, Team 5669, build the competition robot together.

Meet IDEA

Know the few rules

This is a casual class. Safety in the shop is the one thing that is strict.

  • Safety: eye protection whenever anything near you is cut, ground or machined. Closed-toe shoes. Long hair tied back, no loose clothing or jewelry.
  • No machine until you are trained on it. Earbuds are not hearing protection. Horseplay in the shop means you are out for the day.
  • Tell me about any injury, near miss or broken tool, right away. Reporting one is never a problem. Hiding one is.
  • Deadlines are firm. An excused absence moves a due date by the days you were out.
  • AI: use it as much as you want.

Armory

Know how Armory shares files

  • Opening a part in SolidWorks checks it out to you. Everyone else sees who has it and can still open it to look.
  • Saving and closing checks it in. Your save becomes the newest version on every computer in the project.
  • Every check in is kept, so an old version is never lost.
  • Leaving class? Save and close first, or your team waits until you are back.

Nothing to try yet. You will do this for real once you have files.

Meet the tools

xTool StudioGets your dogtag ready for the laser. I run the cut.
Bambu StudioSlices your part for the 3D printers. You run the printers.
How a print goes
  1. SolidWorks: save your part as a STEP file.
  2. Bambu Studio: set the printer and the nozzle, then slice.
  3. Export the sliced plate file to the printer's microSD card or USB drive. Never press Print plate.
