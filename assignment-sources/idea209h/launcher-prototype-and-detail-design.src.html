Prototype · Oct 29 · One class

Answer one question with a rough rig

  1. Each role owner writes the one question their part must answer.
  2. As a team, build a rig or two: cardboard, scrap, tape, clamps, hand tools.
  3. Test at low power, aimed flat. Measure a number: feet, jams, the gear.
  4. Write the result and what it changes. Then the rig goes in the bin.

Four questions, each answered with a number. Still building at the end of class? It was too big.

From a drawer, not a launcher: "Does a 1/4 in gap stop the rubbing? Cardboard spacers at 1/8, 1/4, 3/8 in; 1/4 in had no rub in 20 pushes, so the plates sit 1/4 in apart."

How the calculator works
DragA foam ball slows fast. The calculator flies it in small time steps with air drag (FUEL: 5.91 in, about 0.47 lb, drag coefficient taken as 0.5).
Stored energyEverything except spinning designs works the same way: energy stored, times the percent that reaches the ball, shared between the ball and whatever moves with it. Speed comes from that energy.
Motor speedThe model number is on the drill's or motor's label. Search it with "no load speed". The calculator assumes 80% of that under load.
FlywheelRule of thumb from FRC teams: one wheel and a hood throws at about half the wheel's rim speed, two wheels at about 90%.
Spinning armThe ball leaves at about the arm tip's speed, less some slip.
Bands, springsEnergy is half the full pull times the stretch, for each one. A spring's pull is its rate (lb per in) times how far you squeeze it. A third of a swinging arm's weight moves with the ball.
TwistEnergy is half the twist at full wind times the wind-up angle. Measure the twist: pull the arm with a luggage scale and multiply by the inches from the pivot.
Falling weightEnergy is the weight times how far it falls. The weight keeps some of it: the closer it hangs to the pivot, compared with the ball, the less it keeps. A sling adds to the ball's distance.
HammerThe head swings down and hits the ball. A foam ball is soft, so it bounces off at about 1.5 times the head's speed at most, less for a light head.
AirThe air expands from the tank down the barrel and pushes on the ball's full width. The calculator assumes it expands fast, the way it does in a real shot, and starts at 30% for valve and leak losses. A barrel longer than the air can fill slows the ball, and the calculator says the length where that starts.
Test throwThrow your prototype, measure where the ball lands, and put it in. The calculator works out the real launch speed and the percent that matches it. A number you measured beats every estimate here.

Detail design · Nov 2 · Short day

Model your part in CAD

  1. In SolidWorks, model your part at the sizes in your numbers box.
  2. Model the faces and holes where it meets the other parts first. Those are the interfaces.
  3. Screenshot the model and paste it in your box.
  4. Frame and mount owner: put every part into one assembly as they come in.

Four parts at their real sizes that meet where your interfaces said.

Detail design · Nov 3

Drawings, machine plan, Sign-off 2

  1. Make a drawing of your part: the views, every dimension needed to make it, the stock and the process.
  2. Write your machine plan: which machine, who runs it, which day from Nov 4 to 10.
  3. Every box filled? Open Team plan, find Sign-offs, and press Request sign-off on Design.
  4. No metal is cut before it is approved. Sent back: fix what it names and request again.

Four drawings someone else could make the parts from, and Design shows Requested.
