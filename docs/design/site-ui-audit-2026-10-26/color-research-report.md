# Retune every color role for both themes

Build the system out of roles, not hues. Each meaning (danger, caution, done, a linking color, an FRC alliance) gets its own small token set (ink, fill, on-fill, tint, edge), and each token has separate values for the graphite and space-white themes. Radix, Apple, Primer, Atlassian and Carbon set those values by hand. Material 3 and Adobe Leonardo derive them by algorithm. Both camps reach the same structure: **the role stays fixed and the lightness moves**. Gate the result with WCAG 2.2 math in both themes (4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for non-text, unrounded), treat APCA as advisory (it is not in WCAG 3), simulate color blindness with the Machado 2009 matrices and set pairwise CIEDE2000 floors, and use Material's codified "disliked dark yellow" rule to catch mud. Measured against those gates on 2026-10-26, IDEA's space-white theme has two concrete defects. Its gold (#715d22) falls inside Material's disliked zone. And because every accent was darkened to nearly the same lightness (CIELAB L* 35 to 41), the warm inks collapse for deuteranopes (gold against amber ΔE00 3.5). The fix is structural: in light mode, colored text can carry at most one warm meaning, and identity has to live in fills, icons and signal words. The learning research backs color most strongly for linking a term in the text to the same element in the diagram, with signaling overall worth **g = 0.53 on retention**. Novices gain the most. That supports keeping no more than five fixed course-wide meanings, keeping no more than seven hues in view at once, and reserving red, orange and yellow for real shop hazards, matching ANSI Z535.

## Six systems agree: fix the role, retune the lightness

No major system ships one hex value per hue for both themes. **Radix** ships parallel 12-step scales in which every step has a job: 1 to 2 are backgrounds, 3 to 5 component fills, 6 to 8 borders, 9 to 10 solids, 11 to 12 text. Step 9, "the purest color in the scale," is identical in light and dark (yellow-9 is #ffe629 in both). Steps 1 to 8 and 11 to 12 are completely re-set, and text steps 11 and 12 are guaranteed APCA Lc 60 and Lc 90 against step 2 ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale), [values](https://unpkg.com/@radix-ui/colors@3.0.0/yellow-dark.css)). **Apple** requires light, dark, and an increased-contrast option for each, so four values per color, and warns that system values "may fluctuate from release to release" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). **Primer** generates separate theme files. Its dark "muted" backgrounds are translucent tints of about 10 to 15% alpha over the canvas (for example #388bfd1a), while its light ones are opaque pale tints ([Primer dark](https://unpkg.com/@primer/primitives@11.10.0/dist/css/functional/themes/dark.css)). **Atlassian** maps every token to a different value per theme, under a `color.[property].[role].[emphasis]` grammar ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color)).

The algorithmic camp makes the same move with arithmetic. **Material 3** picks different tones from one HCT tonal palette: primary is tone 40 in light and 80 in dark, its container 90 and 30, the surface 98 and 6. It also publishes a construction rule worth borrowing: **a tone difference of 40 guarantees 3:1, and 50 guarantees 4.5:1** ([material-color-utilities](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/typescript/hct/hct.ts), [spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/color_spec_2021.ts)). **Leonardo** takes target contrast ratios as its input and re-solves every color when the theme's background lightness changes ([Leonardo](https://github.com/adobe/leonardo/tree/main/packages/contrast-colors)). **Carbon** handles data color the same way. Its categorical palette has separate dark values, and its sequential ramps flip direction, so the darkest step marks the largest value in light themes and the lightest step does so in dark themes ([Carbon](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/main/src/pages/data-visualization/color-palettes/index.mdx)).

| System | How the dark value is made | What stays constant across themes | Status vs. data color |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radix | Separate hand-set 12-step scale | Step 9 solid (brand identity) | Status left to the app |
| Apple HIG | Four values per color, incl. increased contrast | The semantic name | Labels or glyphs required |
| Primer | Separate hex per token; dark tints are alpha | Role names | 17-hue `data-*` family, separate |
| Atlassian | Separate value per token | `warning.bold` #FBC828 in both themes | 8 ordered `chart.categorical` slots |
| Carbon | Lighter IBM step of the same hue family | Diverging palettes | 14-color sequence plus 1 to 5 groups |
| Material 3 | Different HCT tone, same palette | Hue and chroma | Not covered |

The lesson for IDEA is the Radix split. **Recognition lives in the solid fill, which can stay constant. Readability lives in the ink, which must flip.** IDEA green can be one fill value in both themes. The green used for words cannot be. Every system also keeps status colors separate from categorical chart colors. Atlassian adds a third family, accents that "should have no specific meaning" ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color)). IDEA assignments need the same three-way split, described in the sections below.

## Yellows and browns stay clean only when re-hued and split into fill and ink

"Muddy" has no standard definition, but it does have an empirical anchor. In Palmer and Schloss (2010), **dark yellow (olive) and dark orange (brown) were the least preferred of 32 colors**, with "a pronounced minimum around dark yellow" and a trough at chartreuse. The authors explain the result by association with disliked objects such as rotting food (r = +0.893, 80% of variance from one predictor) ([Palmer & Schloss 2010](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/Palmer%26Schloss%282010%29.pdf)). Material turned that finding into code. A color is "disliked" when its **HCT hue is 90 to 111, chroma is above 16 and tone is below 65**, and `fixIfDisliked` lifts it to tone 70 ([dislike_analyzer.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/typescript/dislike/dislike_analyzer.ts)). That rule is the most defensible automated mud detector available, because it is published, numeric, and grounded in preference research.

The design systems avoid mud with two moves. First, **they never ask one yellow to be both a fill and a text color**. A bright fill carries dark text in both themes: Radix names yellow, amber, lime, mint and sky as "designed for dark foreground text," and Atlassian pairs its #FBC828 warning fill with a dedicated dark `text.warning.inverse` ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale), [Atlassian tokens](https://unpkg.com/@atlaskit/tokens/dist/esm/artifacts/themes/atlassian-light.js)). The reason is arithmetic. Apple's default yellow is 1.51:1 on white (computed). Second, the light-mode yellow text value is darkened *and* shifted toward orange. Computed from the published hex values, Radix yellow-11 (#9e6c00), Apple's increased-contrast yellow (#a16a00) and Primer attention (#9a6700) all land at **OKLCH L ≈ 0.55 to 0.57, C ≈ 0.12, hue ≈ 74 to 77**. Their HCT chroma is about 44 to 45 and their HCT tone about 48 to 50. That hue shift, roughly 25° toward orange, is what keeps a darkened yellow reading as ochre instead of olive. Tableau states the underlying risk directly: "any yellow, orange, and red colors can be made to appear brown or tan by manipulating their lightness and chroma" ([Tableau](https://www.tableau.com/blog/colors-upgrade-tableau-10-56782)). In dark mode the treatment reverses. The text value is the bright yellow itself (L ≈ 0.86 to 0.90), and garishness is controlled through the background (low-chroma dark browns or translucent tints), not by dulling the foreground.

Applied to IDEA's own tokens (read from `src/lib/design-system/themes/space-white.css` on 2026-10-26), the space-white gold **#715d22 computes to HCT hue 91, chroma 29, tone 40. That is inside Material's disliked zone.** Its OKLCH hue of 90 sits right at the olive minimum rather than the ochre band the other systems use. Space-white amber #77491b (tone 35.5, chroma 32) is outside Material's narrower hue band, but it is exactly the "dark orange (brown)" that Palmer and Schloss found least liked. Both pass contrast easily (5.4 and 6.4 or better), which shows that **passing contrast does not mean a color looks good**. The graphite values (#c8a848 gold at tone 70, #d08030 amber at tone 61) are clean. One more point is specific to IDEA. Its light surfaces (#f7f9f9 and #fbfcfc) are dim enough that the published Radix and Primer values fall short of 4.5:1 (4.09 to 4.36, computed), so IDEA needs inks slightly darker than the published ones. Candidates computed for this report that clear 4.5:1 on all four space-white surfaces while keeping ochre hue and near-maximum chroma are **gold ink #8c5e09** (OKLCH 0.52 / 0.107 / 75, worst case 4.74:1, HCT chroma 40) and **amber ink #9b5409** (OKLCH 0.52 / 0.121 / 58, worst case 4.81:1). Treat these as starting points to verify in the browser, not final values. The HCT figures come from a CAM16 implementation of Material's default viewing conditions written for this analysis, not from Google's library.

## Equal lightness is the hidden colorblind failure in the light theme

About **8% of males and 0.4 to 0.6% of females of Northern European ancestry** have red-green color vision deficiency, mostly deuteranomaly. The US figure is about 7% of males ([color blindness summary](https://kiwix.lema.org/mdwiki_en_all_2024-03/A/Tritanope)). In a mostly male class of 25, plan on one or two students. No Hispanic or Latino-specific rate was found, so the 8% figure is an upper-end planning number for Bosco Tech, not a measurement. The test method is well established. Simulate protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia with the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) matrices, which culori ships as `filterDeficiencyProt/Deuter/Trit(severity)`, then compute pairwise `differenceCiede2000()` ([culori](https://culorijs.org/api/)). The textbook just-noticeable difference of ΔE 2.3 ([colorjs.io](https://colorjs.io/docs/color-difference)) is far too small for marks on a page. Szafir measured discriminability thresholds roughly **7 times larger** than standard models predict, about 16 Δa* units for 6px points and 9.4 for 6px lines ([Szafir 2017](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/VisColors/17-VIS-ModelingColorDifference.pdf)). A floor of ΔE00 ≥ 10 (≥ 15 for small marks) in normal vision and ≥ 8 under simulation is therefore defensible, but it is a design choice, not a standard.

Running that method over IDEA's seven semantic accents (Machado severity 1.0, computed for this report in a standalone script with the matrices entered by hand, so re-run it in culori before relying on the decimals) gives the following worst pairs:

| Theme | Normal vision | Protan | Deutan | Tritan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Graphite | green/teal 9.4 | green/gold 6.6 | **teal/crimson 4.9** | amber/crimson 6.7 |
| Space-white | green/teal 7.5 | **teal/crimson 4.1** | **gold/amber 3.5**, gold/crimson 3.6 | green/cyan 4.9 |

Two separate problems show up. The first is the one Datawrapper names as the main colorblind failure: colors that differ only in hue at equal brightness ([Datawrapper](http://www.vis4.net/blog/1802-datawrapper-color-blind-check/)). Each space-white accent was darkened just enough to pass contrast, so all seven ended up at L* 35 to 41. **Contrast compliance pursued hue by hue produces an isoluminant palette, and an isoluminant palette is the one CVD flattens.** The cause is a hard limit, not a tuning mistake. Even the corrected warm inks above (#8c5e09 and #9b5409) are ΔE00 0.6 apart under deuteranopia, because 4.5:1 on a near-white surface pins every warm hue to about L 0.52. Fills are not pinned the same way. Radix yellow-9 and orange-9 fills (#ffe629 and #f76b15) stay 18 apart under deuteranopia, because a fill is free to differ in lightness. The practical rule follows: **in light mode, colored text carries at most one warm meaning, and every other distinction rides on fill lightness, an icon or a word.** WCAG 1.4.1 already requires that redundancy at Level A ([W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)).

The second problem shows up in normal vision. Green (OKLCH hue 142), teal (155) and cyan (178) fall within 36° of each other, and green against teal is under ΔE00 10 in both themes. Healey found that sharing a color-name category predicts slower search (Spearman r = 0.821 in his seven-color study) ([Healey 1996](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/download/viz.96.pdf)). With green rationed as the brand and "done" color, assignments should not also use teal as a separate meaning. "In progress" in an assignment should move to blue or be carried by an icon.

## Real anchors carry meaning students already know, but only as fills

Some colors in engineering content are not arbitrary, and using them on purpose buys recognition for free. Only a few have published values. The FIRST guidelines (2020, still the version linked from the current brand page) give **FIRST Blue #0066B3, FIRST Red #ED1C24, FIRST Gray #9A989A, FIRST Black #231F20 and FRC Process Blue #009CD7**, plus FTC orange #F57E25 and FLL purple #662D91 and green #00A651 ([FIRST guidelines](https://www.FIRSTInspires.org/sites/default/files/uploads/resource_library/brand/first-brand-guidelines-2020.pdf)). The 2025 REEFSCAPE season used #ABD8E7, #0083AE, #F5E565 and black ([REEFSCAPE guide](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/2025%20Season/Season%20Assets/FIRST_DIVE-FRC-reefscape-styleguide.pdf)). FRC alliance red and blue have no published screen hex; WPILib treats them only as enum values ([WPILib](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/2025/docs/software/basic-programming/alliancecolor.html)), so FIRST Red and FIRST Blue are the nearest official anchors. For software, VS Code publishes #007ACC and #0098FF ([VS Code](https://code.visualstudio.com/brand)), and GitHub Green is #0FBF3E ([GitHub](https://brand.github.com/foundations/color)). GitHub Green sits in IDEA green's own name category, so it should not appear as a separate meaning in IDEA material.

| Content anchor | Value | Source status | Use in an assignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Danger / stop | Safety Red, approx. #BB2724 | Approx. sRGB of ANSI Munsell 7.5R 4.0/14 | Real injury hazards only |
| Warning | Safety Orange, approx. #DE7D00 | Approx. of 5.0YR 6.0/15 | Medium risk |
| Caution | Safety Yellow, approx. #EFCB00 | Approx. of 5.0Y 8.0/12 | Low risk, equipment damage |
| Notice / mandatory | Safety Blue, approx. #005989 | Approx. of 2.5PB 3.5/10 | Required PPE or settings |
| CAN-High / CAN-Low | Yellow / green wire | WPILib, named colors only | Wiring diagrams |
| Power + / ground | Red / black | WPILib, named colors only | Wiring diagrams |
| SolidWorks sketch | Under-defined blue, defined black, over-defined red, conflict yellow, dangling brown, driven gray | SolidWorks help, names only | CAD screenshots and callouts |
| FEA stress | Jet ramp, dark blue low to dark red (0.5, 0, 0) high | matplotlib `_jet_data` | Simulation results |
| Metals | Aluminum #F5F6F6, iron #C0BEBB, stainless #D6D1CB, titanium #B1AAA2, copper #F7CFBF, brass #F5E4AE | physicallybased.info, linear to sRGB | Material chips (darken first) |

ANSI Z535.1 defines its safety colors in Munsell and CIE xyY, and most fall outside sRGB, so every screen hex is an approximation. The values above are one practitioner's hue-matched, gamut-limited conversions ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/framemaker-discussions/ansi-z535-1-safety-colors-in-frame/m-p/11419795), [In Compliance](https://incompliancemag.com/ansi-z535-1-safety-colors-in-focus/)). The wiring, SolidWorks and FEA rows come from [WPILib CAN wiring](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/docs/hardware/hardware-basics/can-wiring-basics.html), [SolidWorks help](https://help.solidworks.com/2013/English/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Sketch_Geometry_Status.htm), [matplotlib](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matplotlib/matplotlib/main/lib/matplotlib/_cm.py) and the [physicallybased API](https://api.physicallybased.info/materials). PBR metal values are reflectances, so aluminum, silver and zinc all land near white. Keep the hue relationships (copper the only red-orange metal, brass the yellow one, titanium the warm gray) and derive darker swatches from them.

Anchors fail as text the same way yellow does. Computed against IDEA's surfaces, **FRC Process Blue is 2.95:1 on space-white** and **FIRST Blue is 3.16:1 on graphite**. Safety Yellow is 1.50:1 on space-white, and Safety Blue is 2.48:1 on graphite. So each anchor is the role's *fill*, as Radix uses step 9, and its ink is derived per theme. The light-theme ink is darker and the dark-theme ink lighter, at the same hue. Several commonly quoted brand values could not be confirmed from an official source and **should not ship until confirmed**: SolidWorks red (~#DA291C), Autodesk Fusion orange, Bambu Lab green (~#00AE42), Onshape blue, xTool, and YouTube's 2024 red (~#FF0033). Prusa's #FA6831 is confirmed only as live website CSS ([Prusa](https://www.prusa3d.com/page/logos-and-brand-materials_404/)).

## Color helps learning when it links text to picture and marks danger

Signaling works. The largest meta-analysis (103 studies, N = 12,201) found **g+ = 0.53 for retention and 0.33 for transfer**, with lower cognitive load ([Schneider et al. 2018](https://isiarticles.com/bundles/Article/pre/pdf/87157.pdf)). It found no prior-knowledge moderator. A study of secondary students using a digital textbook found the opposite: color coding of matching text and picture elements helped only low-prior-knowledge students, who looked at pictures earlier, and left experts unaffected ([Richter & Scheiter 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-019-09492-3)). The two results reconcile as "never harmful on average, most useful for novices." That fits first-time CAD students well. For seniors and returning FRC members, a lighter cue density is enough.

The best-supported use of color in particular is **linking**: the same color marks the same thing in the words and in the picture. In the closest published match to a shop class, first-year electrical trade apprentices learned a switching circuit. Clicking a paragraph lit the circuit elements it named in matching colors in both text and diagram, and multiple-choice performance favored that condition (F(1,13) = 7.17) ([Kalyuga, Chandler & Sweller 1999](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/Kalyuga99.pdf)). Fault-finding did not improve (F = 0.78). Color coding helps students find and remember elements, not troubleshoot them. Eye tracking with 52 participants showed the mechanism: color-coded learners located matching information faster, and both retention and transfer rose ([Ozcelik et al. 2009](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/4c4f4fae-afe5-4186-b7a3-ea75cb837310/an-eye-tracking-study-of-how-color-coding-affects-multimedia-learning)). Both samples are small (16 and 52), so read the direction of the effect, not its size.

Capacity sets the budget. Healey found that **3- and 5-color displays allowed parallel, preattentive search, while 7 and 9 colors forced serial search** for some targets. Seven well-separated colors is the practical ceiling ([Healey 1996](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/download/viz.96.pdf)). Colorblind-safe qualitative palettes are smaller still: ColorBrewer's only colorblind-safe one tops out at 4 ([EU Data Visualisation Guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/colour-for-categories)). Published palette sizes run from Atlassian's 8 to Carbon's maximum of 14, and Carbon offers curated groups for 1 to 5 known categories ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/data-visualization/color-palettes/)). Together these support a **three-family system**. The *semantic* family is fixed and course-wide, with five meanings at most: danger, caution, notice or required, tip or info, and done in IDEA green. It never changes between documents. The *linking* family is assigned fresh in each document from hues the semantic family does not use (blue, violet, magenta, cyan), so a step's term matches its screenshot. The *anchor* family is used only when the content literally is that thing, such as the blue alliance or the CAN-High wire, and always with a label. **A semantic hue is never reused as a linking color.** That is what lets the legend be learned once.

Color is one channel among several, and the standards that IDEA's students will meet in the shop never use it alone. ANSI Z535.4 pairs each color with a signal word and a fixed contrast color: DANGER is white on red, WARNING black on orange, CAUTION black on yellow, NOTICE italic white on blue ([In Compliance](https://incompliancemag.com/ansi-z535-1-safety-colors-in-focus/)). ISO 3864-2 ranks the same colors as low, medium and high risk ([ISO 3864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3864)). Assignments should copy that pattern exactly: color plus signal word plus icon, with red, orange and yellow callouts reserved for real hazards like the fiber laser. Spending them on "common mistake" dilutes the response students need at the machine. That last point is reasoning from the standards' hierarchy, not a measured effect. Typography and position (step numbers, bold terms, callouts placed next to their step) should carry structure, leaving color free to carry meaning. A secondary summary reports that pictorial cues such as arrows and highlights slightly outperform text cues such as bold ([ACU Inspire](https://inspire.acu.edu.au/articles/signalling)). No research on step numbering or step-type icons was retrieved, so that part of the design rests on convention, not evidence.

## Nine gates an automated palette test can enforce

Every gate below runs on resolved token values for **both themes**, against **every surface the token can sit on**: four in space-white (#fbfcfc, #f7f9f9, #edf1f0, #e8eceb) and the six graphite grounds. Each token is bound to a use class (body, secondary, heading, icon, fill, divider), because the thresholds differ by class. Composite translucent tints over the actual surface before measuring. WCAG says nothing about alpha, so compositing is an implementation choice ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)).

| Gate | Enforceable rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Text ink | ≥ 4.5:1, unrounded `>=` (4.499 fails); ≥ 3:1 only for tokens used solely at ≥ 24px, or ≥ 18.5px bold | [WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) |
| 2. Non-text | Icons, component edges, chart marks, focus rings ≥ 3:1 against adjacent colors | [WCAG 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html) |
| 3. On-fill text | Text on each solid fill ≥ 4.5:1; yellow, amber and lime fills take dark text in both themes | Radix, Atlassian practice |
| 4. APCA (advisory, warn only) | Body \|Lc\| ≥ 75 (90 preferred), secondary ≥ 60, headings ≥ 45, solid icons ≥ 30, dividers ≥ 15; flag large dark-theme fills above Lc 90 | [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html) |
| 5. CVD separation | Machado severity 1 for protan, deutan, tritan; every semantic and linking pair ΔE00 ≥ 10 normal (≥ 15 for small marks) and ≥ 8 simulated, *or* the pair differs ≥ 3:1 in luminance | [culori](https://culorijs.org/api/), [Szafir](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/VisColors/17-VIS-ModelingColorDifference.pdf); thresholds are a design choice |
| 6. Mud | Fail HCT hue 90 to 111, chroma > 16, tone < 65; warn when a warm ink (HCT hue 50 to 111) below tone 65 has chroma < 38 | [Material dislike rule](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/typescript/dislike/dislike_analyzer.ts); chroma floor calibrated here between IDEA's 29 to 32 and the published systems' 44 to 49 |
| 7. Count | ≤ 5 semantic meanings course-wide; ≤ 7 distinct hues rendered in one document view | [Healey 1996](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/download/viz.96.pdf) |
| 8. Brand ration | No linking or anchor slot within ΔE00 10 of IDEA green; green appears only on brand, primary action and done roles | Healey category overlap; IDEA rule |
| 9. Redundancy | Every element whose class carries meaning also has a text node, signal word or icon (DOM check) | [WCAG 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) |

Two cautions apply to the tooling. APCA was dropped from the WCAG 3 draft in July 2023, and the April 2026 Editor's Draft says the contrast algorithm "is yet to be determined" ([Roselli 2026](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html)). That is why gate 4 warns and never fails. The `apca-w3` package also carries a license "with specific limitations and exclusions" ([apca-w3](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3)) that should be read before it goes into a public repository. culori covers gates 1, 2, 3 and 5 with no browser. Gate 6 needs an HCT implementation, and Google's `material-color-utilities` is the reference. The current tokens already fail some gates. Graphite crimson #d95f5f is 4.14:1 on bg1, under the text floor, and graphite violet #7050a8 is 2.45:1 there, so violet stays glyph-only as `--violet-ink` already intends.

## Conclusion

The most useful finding was not in any single source: **the harder a palette works to meet contrast hue by hue, the less it can rely on hue at all.** Pinning every ink to 4.5:1 on a near-white page drives the warm hues to the same lightness, and CVD simulation then flattens them. Two consequences follow. In space-white, colored text is a weak channel, so meaning has to move into fills (which are free to vary in lightness), icons and signal words. And the automated test has to check pairs and use classes, not just tokens one at a time, because nearly every individual space-white token passes contrast while the set fails.

The second shift is in how to think about IDEA green. The Radix pattern lets the brand stay constant as a fill while its ink moves per theme, and the learning research says color pays off when it is scarce and consistent. Rationing green is therefore what makes it work, not just a matter of taste. The near-term work is concrete: retune space-white gold and amber out of the mud zone, retire teal as a separate assignment meaning, adopt the three-family split with a five-meaning semantic legend that matches ANSI Z535, and wire the nine gates into the assignment test so a regression fails in CI rather than in front of a colorblind student.
