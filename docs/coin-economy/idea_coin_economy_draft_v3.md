# IDEA Coin Economy - Fall 2026 (v3: 4-Year Model, Zero-Disruption Pass)

Supersedes v2. Same scoring rubrics for fines and awards (still hold up),
but the purchase side and the underlying time horizon both got rebuilt.

---

## What changed and why

1. **The horizon is 4 years, cumulative, never reset** - not one semester.
   Every price that depends on "how long would this take to earn" got
   rechecked against that, not against a 16-week semester.
2. **Some items are supposed to be almost unreachable.** Eating Pass is a
   status marker, not a motivator with a friendly on-ramp. Priced
   accordingly.
3. **Zero disruption to class flow, no exceptions.** A few items don't get
   repriced, they get cut, because no price fixes what's disruptive by
   nature.
4. **Real point scale for Extra Credit**, from the uploaded IDEA209H rubric
   criteria: 1,050 total semester points.
5. **Real Bambu-specific material cost** for 3D printing, plus a machine-time
   component that wasn't priced at all before.
6. **Eating Pass and 3D Printing both corrected after pushback.** Eating
   Pass was priced against 100% of a theoretical 4-year maximum, which
   ignored that a real student spends on other things along the way. 3D
   printing's time charge compounded with material cost more than it
   should have for coursework infrastructure. Both fixed below.
7. **Eating Pass merged into one tier with conduct rules attached**, and
   several other categories simplified (3D printing to flat size tiers,
   Donate Supplies to flat tiers, two identical floor-value awards merged
   into one) to cut day-to-day tedium.
8. **Debt, Destruction of Property, and Cosso's correction authority
   resolved.** Balance can go negative with no cap but locks out purchasing
   until cleared. Destruction of Property splits into negligent (full
   cost) and accidental (first incident free) tiers, tested objectively
   against whether another rule violation applied. Cosso has full
   correction authority in his own section.
9. **Eating Pass violation fine (5i¢) and Perfect Assignment Score's broad
   scope both confirmed as-is.** Refund policy for existing pass-holders
   corrected to refund-only, no automatic grant of the new pass.
10. **Answer Questions and Quality IDEA Background un-merged.** They
    fire at genuinely different cadences (daily vs. monthly) and now
    different prices (1i¢ vs. 3i¢) - the merge assumed they were the same
    shape of thing, and they weren't. Shop Steward ratio corrected: Junior
    (20 students) gets 2, Sophomore and Senior stay at 1 each.
11. **Major consolidation and repricing pass.** Gambling and Zoned Out
    removed. Emotional Outburst, Arguing, and general disruption merged
    into one Disruptive Behavior fine; four minor compliance fines merged
    into one Classroom Standards Violation. Shop Not Cleaned Up raised to
    full parity with Shop Safety Violation (12i¢). Four 209H unit-specific
    awards removed in favor of universal categories; Platform Milestone
    and Platform Cosmetic Unlock moved from IDEA100-only to Core. Contract
    Completion dropped to 1i¢/hour. 3D Printing reverted from size tiers
    to the slicer's actual reported weight and time. Extra Credit dropped
    to 5i¢/point after confirming no UC rule governs this (it's local
    policy). Eating Pass corrected to 150i¢ using real balance data -
    richest current students sit just over 100i¢ after 2.5 semesters,
    not the ~1,150i¢ ceiling the model assumed.
12. **Second consolidation pass.** Classmate Trust Violation merged
    Framing a Classmate and Tampering With a Classmate's Computer at 5i¢.
    Eating Without a Pass renamed Eating Violation to actually cover both
    no-pass eating and pass-holder rule violations. Flash Drive Not
    Returned retired (untracked, unused); 3D Printer Not Reset absorbed
    the printer-specific case and rose to 3i¢. Above and Beyond widened
    to 1-3i¢. Correct Answer in Class now requires actual reasoning, not
    just a right word. 3D Printing time moved from per-hour to bands, free
    under an hour and free overnight regardless of length. Extra Credit
    cap raised from 8 points (0.76%) to 2% of semester points (21 points
    for 209H, 42i¢ to max out) after weighing 1%/2%/3%/5% cap options, then
    the per-point rate itself dropped again to 2i¢ (from 5i¢) once the
    cap changed, per the "keep the total cost of maxing out roughly
    constant" logic in Part 5. Full allowance costs 42i¢, not 105i¢.
14. **Earning-rate recompute done.** Replaced the synthetic ~9i¢/wk gross
    model with a rate built off real balance data: ~2i¢/wk moderate,
    ~3i¢/wk highly engaged, ~5i¢/wk if also holding a role. Checked Eating
    Pass, Pay Raise, and Extra Credit against it - all three hold up,
    nothing needed to reprice. Part 6's weeks-to-afford columns rebuilt
    with the corrected rate.
15. **Remaining open items closed.** Eating Pass strike count and Outside
    Time both confirmed. Song Request reactivated at 3i¢ now that an
    approval-gated Spotify design (Part 8) solves its disruption problem.
    Balance carryover resolved as a policy question - it just needed the
    account-linking tool, now specified for the website chat: attach a
    balance to an email regardless of login status, so it's already there
    the moment a student first logs in rather than something linked
    reactively afterward. No conversion math needed since it's the same
    currency throughout.

## Part 1: The Framework (revised)

### 1a. Time horizon: 4 years, not a semester

Working estimate: roughly 130 active weeks across all 4 years (144 weeks if
every year were a full 36-week year at full engagement, minus IDEA100's
freshman-year shortfall and the other carve-out you mentioned). **This is my
estimate of your math, not a number you gave me directly - correct it and
every ceiling below moves with it.**

Balances and wage tiers never reset between years or between courses. A
student's IDEA100 balance in September of freshman year is the same account
they're still spending from as a senior.

### 1b. Weekly earning rates - recomputed against real data

This replaces the original synthetic model. Real anchor: richest current
students, after up to 2.5 semesters (~40 weeks), sit just over 100i¢ -
roughly 2.5i¢/week for a genuine top performer under the old system. Two
things changed between then and now: Contract Completion is down to
1i¢/hour (below what historical contracts actually averaged), and Weekly
Role Stipend is real new income that never existed in practice - Roles
never launched, so none of that 100i¢ included any role income at all.

| | Moderate | Highly engaged, no role | Highly engaged, with a role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate | ~2i¢/wk | ~3i¢/wk | ~5i¢/wk |

Role slots are scarce (1-2 per section) - most engaged students, even
excellent ones, won't hold one. "Highly engaged, no role" is the right
default for pricing anything meant to be reachable by the most responsible
students broadly, not just the handful who also land one of very few
positions.

### 1c. What the recompute actually changes

Checked every currently-active price against the corrected rate:

- **Eating Pass (150i¢):** at ~3i¢/wk, ~50 weeks - longer than the 40
  weeks it took to reach the real historical 100i¢ mark, which is the
  right relationship (150i¢ should take longer than 100i¢ did). Holds.
- **Pay Raise (40i¢, tier 1):** at ~3i¢/wk, ~13 weeks - a real commitment,
  not pointless. Holds; only the framing text in Part 4 cited the old
  inflated rate and needed a correction, not the price itself.
- **Extra Credit (42i¢ full allowance):** at ~3i¢/wk, ~14 weeks -
  proportionate. Holds.

Nothing else needed to move. The earlier corrections - mainly Eating Pass,
built directly off your real balance data rather than this model - had
already absorbed most of the damage the original inflated rate was doing.
This mainly closes the open item rather than surfacing new problems.

### 1d/1e. Fine and Award rubrics

Unchanged from v2 - still 3S+2T+D+3R for fines, E+V+2A for awards, same
price table. These didn't need fixing, only the purchase-side time horizon
did.

### 1f. Purchases: real-cost anchors updated, 3D printing corrected

3D printing prices off **Bambu Lab's own store rate** (~$25/kg for
Bambu-brand filament, verified against current 2026 store and third-party
pricing, not generic market ABS) plus a **separate, much lighter
machine-time charge** than the first pass had. The original two-part
formula compounded badly on anything beyond a quick, small print, which
matters because 3D printing isn't a luxury item like Eating Pass - it's a
tool students need for actual coursework (209H's Unit 5 build, IDEA100
projects, FRC-adjacent work). It should stay close to real-cost recovery,
not scale up the way a deliberately-exclusive purchase does.

## Part 2: Fines (Core - unchanged except Property Damage)

| Fine | S | T | D | R | Score | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shop Safety Violation | 3 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 10 | 12i¢ |
| Shop Not Cleaned Up | 3 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 10 | 12i¢ |
| Coin Theft | 0 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 11 | 12i¢ |
| Mint Tampering (Suspect Known) | 0 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 11 | 12i¢ |
| Classmate Trust Violation | 0 | 2 | 3 | 0 | 7 | 5i¢ |
| Eating Violation | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 7 | 5i¢ |
| Leaving Without Permission | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 6 | 5i¢ |
| Disruptive Behavior (covers outbursts, arguing, general disruption) | 1 | 0 | 2 | 0 | 5 | 3i¢ |
| 3D Printer Not Reset (includes the printer's flash drive not returned) | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 4 | 3i¢ |
| Long Bathroom Break | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 2i¢ |
| Off-Task Device Use | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 2i¢ |
| Unauthorized Printing | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2i¢ |
| Classroom Standards Violation (background, seating, furniture, general tidiness, flash drives other than the printer's) | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1i¢ |

**Classmate Trust Violation** merged Framing a Classmate and Tampering
With a Classmate's Computer at your call - both are the same underlying
thing, a targeted betrayal of a specific classmate's trust, whether it's
fabricating evidence against them or interfering with their work. One
category, 5i¢.

**Eating Violation** renamed from Eating Without a Pass to actually match
what it covers: eating with no pass at all, or eating badly enough with a
pass to trigger a strike (odor, mess, wrong timing - see Part 4's conduct
rules). Same fine either way, since both are "not exercising the
privilege correctly."

**Flash Drive Not Returned removed** - untracked, unused. If it's the 3D
printer's flash drive, that's a 3D Printer Not Reset violation now (raised
to 3i¢ - the printer isn't ready for the next student either way, whether
because it wasn't reset or because its drive walked off). Any other flash
drive falls under Classroom Standards Violation.

**Disruptive Behavior** absorbed Emotional Outburst and Arguing - all three
were different flavors of the same underlying thing, and Emotional Outburst
was the most serious of the three, so the merged category holds at that
level (3i¢) rather than the lower 2i¢ the plain "disruption" case used to
carry alone.

**Classroom Standards Violation** absorbed Inappropriate Desktop
Background, Out of Seat for Attendance, Feet on Another Chair, and
Classroom Left Messy - four separate 1i¢ categories that were all the same
kind of thing: a small, easy-to-fix compliance lapse with no safety or
trust dimension. One name, one price, covers all of them plus anything
similar that comes up later without needing a new line item every time.

**Shop Not Cleaned Up** re-scored at full parity with Shop Safety
Violation (S=3, not 1). Tools and materials left out in a working shop are
a real hazard for whoever uses the space next, not just an untidiness
issue - the same category of risk as the safety violation itself, so it
should cost the same.

**Property Damage - split into Careless and Accident tiers.** No real
cash from a student, ever, in either case. The test is objective, not a
judgment call: did another rule violation apply at the same time (Shop
Safety Violation, horseplay, using equipment without required
certification or permission)?

*Property Damage (Careless)* - another violation applied: flat 3i¢
behavioral fine, plus the coin-denominated exchange rate on full
repair/replacement cost - **1i¢ = $0.25.** A $10 mishap adds 40i¢ on top
of the 3i¢ base; a $50 one adds 200i¢.

*Property Damage (First Accident)* - proper, permitted use, no other
violation: no charge for a student's first accidental incident in a
semester - logged, not billed.

*Property Damage (Repeat Accident)* - same student, second+ accidental
incident in the same semester: flat 5i¢, unrelated to replacement cost - a
pattern worth a conversation, not a debt. A third is worth a direct
conversation about whether that student keeps unsupervised access to that
equipment, which isn't something pricing solves.

Reserve the exchange-rate portion of the Careless tier for genuinely
costly damage - a scuffed table doesn't need it, a broken tool or a ruined
print does.

**Mechanism, not a fine:** Mint Tampering (Suspect Unknown) - section-wide
mint privilege freeze until resolved, not a coin amount.

**Debt.** Balance can go negative with no cap - fines keep applying past
zero. While a balance is negative, no purchases of any kind are allowed,
from any category. Earning continues normally and pays down the negative
balance first; purchasing resumes once the balance clears zero. Debt
carries forward the same way a positive balance does - no resets between
years or courses.

## Part 3: Awards (Core - several revised)

| Award | Price | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Highest Grade in Section (Weekly) | **1i¢/week** | Per your correction: weekly, matches the wage exactly - the top student that week gets double pay, not a rare achievement bonus. |
| Perfect Score on Graded Work | **round(points ÷ 25)i¢, min 1i¢** | Scales with what the piece is actually worth in any class: a 25pt quiz = 1i¢, a 50pt lab = 2i¢, a 100pt test or project = 4i¢. Applies broadly to any individually-graded piece - this is the one category that already covers excellent unit-specific work without needing a separate named award for every unit. |
| Donate Supplies (Tissue Box merged in) | **Small ~$1-5 value = 1i¢, Medium ~$5-15 = 3i¢, Large $15+ = case-by-case, your sign-off** | Simplified from a live $-per-3 calculation to three flat tiers - same logic (a tissue box shouldn't pay like a case of gloves) without doing math on every donation. |
| Correct Answer in Class | 1i¢ (floor), capped at once/day | Quality bar added: correct **and** actually reasoned - the student explains why, not just lands on the right word. A right answer with no real thinking behind it, or a lucky guess, doesn't qualify. Un-merged from Micro-Recognition, fires daily, stays at the floor rate. |
| Quality Desktop Background | **3i¢**, resets monthly regardless of when in the month it was last submitted | Un-merged and repriced: E=1 (setup effort), V=1 (contributes to classroom professionalism), A=1 (checked monthly, not everyone sustains it) = score 4, a band above the floor. Fixed monthly reset date, not a rolling 30-day window from last submission. |
| Above and Beyond | **1-3i¢**, instructor discretion | Widened the floor down from 2i¢ - not every above-and-beyond moment is worth the same as a Documentation Packet used to be. Still where genuine peer-teaching moments live. |

**Does stacking weekly bonuses make sense?** Yes, and it's meant to. Base
Wage (1i¢), Weekly Role Stipend (2i¢), and Highest Grade in Section (1i¢)
measure three different things - enrollment, ongoing responsibility, and
that week's academic performance - so a student who's a role-holder *and*
has the best grade that week legitimately earns all three: 1+2+1 = 4i¢
that week, not a capped or averaged number. It's not "triple wage," it's
three separate, independently-earned things happening to land on the same
student at once. Say if you'd rather cap the weekly total instead.

**Removed:** Helping Peers (abuse-prone, self-reportable), Gambling (folded
- see Disruptive Behavior in Part 2 for the general-conduct version of
this concern), Zoned Out (your call, dropped entirely).

**No fixed amount, by design:** Competition Winnings. Purely
tournament-dependent - award it case by case when it happens, same as
Contract Completion.

**Unchanged structure, repriced:** Contract Completion. Base Payout =
**1i¢ per estimated hour** (down from 2i¢), +50% for specialized skill
over general labor. Infrastructure-improvement work (fixing or building
parts of the coin system itself) prices the same way - it's a type of
contract, not a separate category.

**Mechanism, not an award:** Physical Coin Submission (credited at whatever
is submitted), Balance Correction and Refund (Cosso has full authority to
issue both in his own section, same as you in yours - corrections
shouldn't have to route through you to function day to day), Weekly Wage
(the anchor, 1a).

**3D Printing time bands, in full.** Material is always 1i¢ per 10g off
the slicer's exact reported weight - that part didn't change. Time:

- Under 1 hour: no time charge at all.
- 1-3 hours: 2i¢.
- 3-6 hours: 4i¢.
- 6+ hours: 6i¢.
- **Scheduled overnight** (started after the last class period ends,
  finished before the next school day) - no time charge, any length.

The overnight rule is the "smart about it" case you're describing: a
long print started overnight isn't taking the machine away from anyone
who'd otherwise be using it, so there's no real opportunity cost to
charge for. Using a fixed, checkable window (after last period, done
before school resumes) instead of "was anyone else actually going to
print" keeps this a yes/no fact instead of a judgment call.

**Eating Pass - merged, with conduct rules attached.** One pass now, not
two - it functions as the old Executive tier (eat in the classroom/shop
during independent work time), but comes with real conditions, since
exclusivity breaks down if holding it just means unrestricted eating:

- No strong-odor food - nothing that lingers in a room full of electronics
  (fast food, reheated leftovers, that kind of thing).
- Clean completely after eating - no crumbs, wrappers, or residue left at
  the station.
- Only during independent work time, never during instruction or a
  demonstration.

Violating any of these while holding the pass triggers the existing Eating
Violation fine (5i¢) for that instance - reusing the fine that already
exists rather than inventing a new one. A third violation in a semester
revokes the pass permanently, no refund: earned once, but it can still be
lost. Strike count confirmed at 3.

**Transition for existing pass-holders.** Anyone who already bought Basic
or Executive under the old two-tier system gets refunded what they paid -
nothing more. The new single pass isn't automatically granted; they earn
and buy it at 150i¢ like anyone else, same bar as everyone else now
clears. Actually crediting the refund is a ledger action, not a pricing
one, and folds into the balance carryover / reconciliation work already
flagged as its own pass.

## Part 5: Course-specific layers

Pared back this round: universal categories (Perfect Score on Graded Work,
Above and Beyond, Contract Completion) already cover excellent work in any
unit of any class without needing a separate named award every time a new
unit gets added. Only genuinely structural, non-curriculum-tied items stay
course-specific below.

### IDEA100-only

Nothing left here - Platform Milestone and Platform Cosmetic Unlock both
moved to Core (Parts 3 and 4) since platform engagement isn't actually
IDEA100-exclusive. This layer stays open for anything that turns out to be
genuinely specific to the IDEA100 rotation once that curriculum is locked
in the other chat.

### 209H-only

| Reason | Price |
|---|---|
| Weekly Role Stipend (Shop Steward / Quartermaster / Safety Officer / Lab Tech) | 2i¢/week while holding the role |

**Removed this round:** Documentation Packet Submitted, Design Revised for
Manufacturing, Station Safety Sign-off, and Integration Handoff. All four
were tied to a specific 209H unit rather than being something that applies
to any class - per your call, this chat is only pricing universal items
from here on. Excellent work in any of those units still earns coin
through Perfect Score on Graded Work and Above and Beyond, which already
scale to whatever the piece is worth without a dedicated line item. On
Integration Handoff specifically: that was my own addition from an earlier
rebuild, not something you'd asked for - dropping it rather than
re-explaining it.

**Roles system - recommend launching this fall.** The Role Questions sheet
already has a full application quiz written for all four roles and it's
never gone live. Application fee: 0i¢, the free-response question is the
real gate. Shop Steward: 1 for Sophomore (10 students) and 1 for Senior (11
students), 2 for Junior (20 students, close enough to the quiz's original
25-student assumption to support a second). Quartermaster: 1 per section
regardless of size. Safety Officer and Lab Tech ratios weren't specified in
the quiz - propose 1-2 per section, your call.

**Extra Credit (per point) - cap confirmed, rate now settled too.** From
the rubric doc: 1,050 total semester points. On the UC-limit question:
investigated, and there isn't one - UC's honors designation governs GPA
weighting and course-approval criteria, not how a teacher composes a
grade internally, so there's no external percentage to defer to. That
made the cap entirely a local call.

**Cap options that were weighed, for the record:**

| Cap | Points (of 1,050) | Full cost at 2i¢/pt | What it can actually do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% | ~11 pts | 22i¢ | Barely more than the old 8pt cap - probably still too small to swing a borderline grade, same complaint as before. |
| **2% (chosen)** | ~21 pts | **42i¢** | Enough to move a grade a couple points - could tip an 89% to 91%, a real B+/A- boundary case. Costs a genuinely substantial chunk of a semester's earning. |
| 3% | ~31 pts | 62i¢ | Could cross a full letter-adjacent boundary on its own (89%→92%). Starts to feel like it's doing real work rather than marginal cleanup. |
| 5% | ~52 pts | 104i¢ | Large enough to meaningfully substitute for actual performance on a chunk of coursework - risks undercutting what "honors" is supposed to certify. |

2% was the smallest cap that actually does what "8 points doesn't seem
like enough to matter" was pointing at - move a real, borderline grade
decision - without crossing into "extra credit is doing the work instead
of the student." Framed as a percentage rather than a fixed point count so
it travels cleanly to other IDEA classes with different point totals, not
just 209H's 1,050.

**Rate: 2i¢/point** (down from 5i¢). Derivation: the total cost of maxing
out extra credit had already been settled at 40i¢ (5i¢ x the old 8-point
cap) before the cap changed - nothing about that total ever felt wrong,
only the cap size did. Holding that total roughly steady and dividing by
the new 21-point cap gives 40 ÷ 21 ≈ 1.9i¢/point, rounded to a clean 2i¢.
Above the old 1i¢/point (not a return to what was already shown to be
exploitable), well below the 5i¢ that felt disconnected from a single
point's actual grade value.

- **Cap: 2% of total semester points** - 21 points for 209H specifically,
  **42i¢** to max out.
- **Restricted to Unit Labs, Unit Assignments, and Documentation checks
  only.** Not purchasable against the Integrated Design Challenge or the
  Final Exam.
- The cap is still doing more integrity work than the price - a student
  banking coins across semesters could otherwise afford almost any rate
  off accumulated wealth rather than current effort, which is why the
  hard point ceiling matters regardless of which rate sits under it.

## Part 8: Out of scope here - flag to the website chat

**Admin tool: attach a balance to an email, independent of login.** The
part that matters most: you (or Cosso, in his own sections) need to be
able to attach a student's existing balance/history to their email at any
time - before they've ever logged in, not only after. So when a student
does log in for the first time, their balance is just already there,
correct, with nothing left for you to do at that moment. That means a
balance record has to be keyable by email alone, not something that only
gets created once a login happens - a first login should simply resolve
to whatever balance already exists for that email, automatically, no
manual re-linking step required right then. Retroactive linking should
still work as a fallback for anyone who logs in before you've gotten to
them, but pre-provisioning ahead of time is the primary case, especially
given how many students are about to onboard at once - worth checking
whether the tool should support attaching several at a time rather than
one email at a time. This is the actual mechanism that makes
reconciliation work, not just a nice-to-have - it resolves the
balance-carryover question flagged since the first draft of this
document. The policy side is simple enough that the tool doesn't need
much logic beyond the link itself: whatever the old balance was becomes
the starting new balance, no conversion math, since it's the same
currency throughout. Any physical coins a student is still holding that
were never logged anywhere get credited the normal way once linked -
Physical Coin Submission already covers that, nothing new to build for
it. Ledger and auth engineering, lives in the website chat - take it
there directly. Given real value sits behind every
balance, "tight as Fort Knox" is the right bar for this build.

**Song Request - approval-gated Spotify requests.** Website feature: a
student submits a track (must resolve to a valid, playable Spotify track),
it lands in a pending queue, nothing plays until you approve it - no
auto-play, ever. School-appropriateness needs a first-pass filter on
Spotify's own explicit-content flag, but that's a floor, not a substitute
for your review; you're still the actual gate. Smooth on both ends is the
design target: fast to submit, fast to scan and approve, and some way for
an approved request to actually reach whatever you're playing through
without you manually re-adding it - that last part is worth figuring out
early since it determines whether this is genuinely low-friction or just
paperwork with extra steps. This is the build that makes Song Request
non-disruptive, which is what's letting it go active again below.

**Correction audit trail.** Now that Cosso and you both have independent
Balance Correction authority, the ledger should log who made each
correction and why, not just the resulting number. Also for the website
chat, not something to solve here.
