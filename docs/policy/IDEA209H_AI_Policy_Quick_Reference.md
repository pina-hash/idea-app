# IDEA209H AI Use Policy - Instructor Quick Reference

**For:** IDEA209H instructors, all sections.
**Source:** condensed from the pathway-wide `IDEA_AI_Use_Policy.md`. Self-contained. Nothing else required.

---

## Why this exists

Not a restrictive policy. Some students will struggle badly without AI, and the point is helping them use it well, not policing them away from it. These are fast-paced classes, and a stuck student needs to be able to get themselves moving again without waiting on one instructor to reach them. AI helps a student learn the material. It doesn't replace learning it. Everything below exists to keep that line clear.

---

## The rule everything else hangs on

You already run this. It's the Presentation and Defense requirement already written into the approved course description: every numerical value and selection decision gets challenged live, and a student who can't defend a value or a decision has to correct it before the work earns credit.

That's the backstop for AI use too. A student who leaned on AI without understanding what it gave them fails the defense the exact same way a student with bad arithmetic fails it. You don't need to detect AI use. You need to keep running the defense the way you already do.

---

## The four levels

Every assignment or checkpoint is marked with one of these:

| Level | Rule |
|---|---|
| 0 · OFF | No AI in producing the work. Notebook entries, timed work, the defense itself, exams. |
| 1 · COACH | AI as tutor. Explains a concept, explains why an approach is wrong, walks through a method. Doesn't generate any part of the submitted answer. |
| 2 · ASSIST | AI can help generate, draft, search, or explore. Everything factual gets checked against a real source. Student can explain and defend every part. |
| 3 · OPEN | Unrestricted. Rare, only when using AI well is itself the point of the assignment. Always labeled explicitly on the assignment, never assumed. |

---

## Where each unit's work usually falls

| Kind of work | Usual level | What "verified" means here |
|---|---|---|
| Force/torque/gear-ratio calculations, tolerance and fit | 1 · COACH | Redo it independently. Check units. Check the magnitude makes sense. |
| Component and material selection, bill of materials | 2 · ASSIST | The spec is checked against the real datasheet or catalog page, not AI's memory of one. |
| Written conclusions and lab reports | 2 · ASSIST | Any cited fact or standard is confirmed real. |
| Self-assessment or reflective writing | 1 · COACH | The voice and the thinking have to be the student's. AI reviews a real draft, doesn't write it. |
| Notebook entries, timed work | 0 · OFF | Enforced by being in the room, not by the honor system. |

Assignments Mr. Pina builds show the level for each module as a small badge in the header (`AI · COACH`, etc.), color-coded, with the full rule available on hover. No badge on a module means it's a live or in-person component, like the defense, where a badge doesn't apply.

## What students submit

Every assignment ends with a short declaration before the submission checklist:

> I completed this assignment honestly. Everything submitted is my own work, including anywhere AI helped, disclosed below. I can explain any part of it if asked.

Followed by a disclosure question: how they used AI, picked from didn't use it / talked through a concept or checked finished work I'd already done / used it to help generate, draft, or search and verified it / other. Anything past the first option opens one or two short required lines naming what it was used for and, for the third option, what they checked it against.

Worth glancing at when you grade, not something to audit line by line. It's there so "I used AI for this part" is a normal thing for a student to write down, not a confession.

---

## When something's off

Same as any other correction: fix it before it earns credit, not a zero. A student who leaned on AI more than the level allowed, or forgot to disclose it, gets asked to redo the affected part. That's it, most of the time.

Escalate to an actual integrity conversation only when both are true: the student didn't disclose it, and they can't explain it when you ask them directly. That combination is what actually tells you something dishonest happened. Either one alone (disclosed but the work is weak, or undisclosed but they can explain it fine when you push) is a redo.
