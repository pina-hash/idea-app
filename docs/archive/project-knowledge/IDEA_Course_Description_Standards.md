### The Math Co-Requisite Rule
All Area D courses must have at least one year of elementary algebra as a pre-or-co-requisite. List **Integrated Math I (Co-requisite, Required)** for all IDEA pathway courses. This is non-negotiable for portal advancement.

### Honors Title Rule
If a course carries UC Honors Designation Yes, the word "Honors" must appear in both the Title field and the Transcript Abbreviation field. The portal honors designation alone does not surface the word "Honors" on a student transcript.

### Transition Year Prerequisite Rule
When a new prerequisite course is being introduced simultaneously with the course that requires it, list the prerequisite as Recommended rather than Required. This prevents the portal from technically barring the first cohort of students who could not have completed a course that did not exist yet. The honors credit distinction for students without the Area D prerequisite is handled at the school transcript level.

### The Block Schedule Credit Statement
Every course overview must open with this sentence, adapted to the actual hour count:

*"Operating on an accelerated block schedule, this course meets daily for 90 minutes over one semester, delivering [X] instructional hours equivalent to a full-year Carnegie unit."*

Hour counts by course:
- Semester 1 courses (IDEA114, IDEA209, IDEA305, IDEA404): approximately 135 hours
- Semester 2 courses (IDEA306): approximately 142 hours
- Quarter courses (IDEA210): approximately 72 hours (equivalent to a semester-length course)

## 2. SECTION-BY-SECTION WRITING STANDARDS

### Course Overview
- **Sentence count:** 5-7 sentences. No exceptions.
- **First sentence:** Always the block schedule credit statement.
- **Content:** Overarching content and goals of the entire course. Does not describe specific unit content in detail.
- **Must include:** Lab time percentage. Mathematical/quantitative reasoning. Instructional approach if distinctive.
- **Must signal all three Area D UC criteria explicitly:**
  - Students ask questions, formulate hypotheses, and construct explanations
  - Students collect and analyze quantitative data and apply mathematical reasoning
  - Students draw evidence-based conclusions and communicate findings in writing
- **Must include for honors courses:** Explicit statement of what distinguishes this course as honors-level.
- **Must not include:** References to honors rigor in a non-honors course. Specific assignment names. Grade level assumptions. Administrative framing.

### Lab Time Percentage
The lab time percentage covers all teacher-supervised hands-on work in which students design, fabricate, measure, analyze, and document their engineering process. This includes fabrication sessions with engineering notebook documentation, not only investigation-style lab activities. Pure SolidWorks modeling sessions without a data collection or testing component do not count toward lab time.

### Program Name Definition
On first mention in any course description, define the program as "the Integrated Design Engineering and Art (IDEA) pathway." Subsequent references may use IDEA alone.

### Unit Summary
- **Sentence count:** 5-7 sentences. No exceptions.
- **Content:** Describes what students learn and do in the unit. Answers "What are your students learning in this unit?"
- **Must include:** At least one explicit mention of quantitative analysis, calculation, or mathematical reasoning. At least one engineering practice from the NGSS list. Named tools, processes, or standards where relevant.
- **Must not include:** Assignment descriptions - deliverables, submission logistics, approval workflows, or grading criteria. Lab descriptions. The moment a summary describes how an assignment works rather than what students learn, it has crossed into assignment territory.

### Unit Assignment
- **Sentence count:** 5-7 sentences. No exceptions.
- **One primary deliverable:** Describe one key product. If multiple activities exist, identify the most significant as primary and reference others briefly.
- **Do not open with administrative framing:** Sentences like "X is the primary assessment for this unit" waste the sentence budget. Start with what students actually do.
- **Content:** Names what students produce. Describes how they are evaluated.
- **Must not include:** Lab descriptions. Multiple disconnected deliverables. Logistics from the unit summary.

### Unit Lab (Area D Required)
- **Sentence count:** 5-7 sentences. No exceptions.
- **One write-up product:** Each lab section describes one investigation with one written deliverable.
- **No meta-commentary:** Do not end lab sections with editorial statements about what the lab "represents," "demonstrates," or "develops." Describe what students do, collect, and conclude.
- **Safety language:** Must be present but must not be a standalone abrupt sentence. Fold safety into the activity description naturally. Examples:
  - Poor: "Safe tool handling and eye protection are required throughout."
  - Better: "...measuring dimensions and mass with eye protection observed throughout."
  - Better: "...taking care around any sharp edges during measurement."
  - The safety mention should be specific to the actual hazard present in that lab, not generic.
- **Content:** Hands-on investigation with inquiry, observation, analysis, and write-up. Must be a genuine investigation, not a safety orientation, notebook compilation, or demonstration.
- **Must include:** A clear inquiry question or hypothesis. Physical hands-on activity with named equipment. Quantitative data collection. Analysis including calculations. A formal written product.

## 3. AREA D COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST

### Structural Requirements
- [ ] Course overview: 5-7 sentences
- [ ] Every unit has a summary: 5-7 sentences each
- [ ] Every unit has an assignment: 5-7 sentences each
- [ ] Every unit has a lab: 5-7 sentences each
- [ ] Lab time percentage stated explicitly in overview
- [ ] Lab time percentage reflects ALL hands-on work, not only investigation labs
- [ ] Block schedule credit statement in first sentence of overview
- [ ] All three Area D UC criteria signaled in overview
- [ ] Math co-requisite listed (Integrated Math I, Required)
- [ ] IDEA program defined on first mention
- [ ] Course materials section complete (textbooks, websites, software, equipment)
- [ ] Standards section present for internal compliance verification

### Content Requirements (All 8 NGSS Practices Must Appear)
- [ ] Asking questions and defining problems (requirements analysis, problem formulation)
- [ ] Developing and using models (SolidWorks, simulation, analytical models)
- [ ] Planning and carrying out investigations (lab procedures, test plans)
- [ ] Analyzing and interpreting data (inspection data, performance data, test results)
- [ ] Using mathematics and computational thinking (force/torque calculations, gear ratios, etc.)
- [ ] Constructing explanations and designing solutions (design rationale, engineering analysis)
- [ ] Engaging in argument from evidence (design reviews, peer challenges, justified decisions)
- [ ] Obtaining, evaluating, and communicating information (technical writing, presentations, datasheets)

### Lab Quality Requirements
- [ ] At least one lab per unit is a genuine hands-on investigation
- [ ] At least one lab across the course is student-designed (student formulates hypothesis or procedure)
- [ ] Safety addressed in every lab section, folded into activity naturally (not standalone)
- [ ] Every lab has quantitative data collection
- [ ] Every lab has exactly one formal written product
- [ ] No lab section ends with editorial meta-commentary

### Assignment Quality Requirements
- [ ] Each unit assignment describes one primary deliverable
- [ ] No assignment section opens with administrative framing
- [ ] No assignment section describes logistics that belong in the unit summary
- [ ] Assessment criteria or evaluation language present in every assignment section

### Assessment Variety Requirements
- [ ] At least two assessment types present: written exam or quiz, lab report, formal presentation, project portfolio, engineering analysis report

### Redundancy Check
- [ ] Review each unit lab and assignment against the preceding course (IDEA114 for IDEA209, IDEA209 for IDEA210, etc.) to confirm the activity is genuinely advancing the skills introduced earlier and not simply repeating them at the same level.

## 4. HONORS COURSE REQUIREMENTS

### Eligibility Rules
- Course must target 10th grade or higher. No 9th-grade course is eligible.
- Course must require at least one year of prior college-preparatory laboratory science. The Area D course immediately preceding in the sequence satisfies this.
- Course must demonstrate rigor comparable to AP or IB coursework.
- "Honors" must appear in both the Title field and the Transcript Abbreviation field.

### Additional Required Elements
- **Honors differentiation language** in the course overview. Must explicitly state what distinguishes this as honors-level.
- **Comprehensive written final examination** covering laboratory concepts and skills across the full course. Non-negotiable Area D honors requirement. Must appear as a distinct section in the submission.
- **Culminating project** may be part of the honors final examination requirement but not all of it. A written exam component must exist alongside it.
- **No honors language** in any non-honors course description.

### Final Assessment Section Format
The Final Assessment section must describe:
1. That a comprehensive written examination is administered
2. The scope of the exam (which units are covered)
3. The type of problems or questions students face
4. How laboratory concepts and skills are specifically tested
5. How the culminating project relates to the exam (supplemental, not replacement)

## 5. WRITING STYLE RULES

### Punctuation
- No em dashes under any circumstances.
- No hyphens used as separators. Hyphens appear only in hyphenated words.
- No semicolons used to chain independent clauses. Write two sentences instead.
- No colons used as separators between two independent clauses. "X puts that principle into practice: students do Y" must become two sentences.
- List colons are acceptable only when the items after the colon are noun phrases, not independent clauses. Using "including" instead is always cleaner and avoids ambiguity.

### Sentence Length
- Target: 15-28 words per sentence.
- Hard ceiling: 35 words. Any sentence over 35 words must be split.
- Variety: Mix short and medium sentences. Consecutive short sentences can be as jarring as long ones. Aim for natural flow.
- Engineering noun phrases run longer than other subjects, but this does not justify long sentences. A sentence over 35 words almost always contains two ideas that should be split.

### Sentence Structure
- Avoid opening every sentence with "Students." Vary the subject.
- Avoid ending every unit section the same way. Each unit should conclude differently.
- Avoid stacking subordinate clauses. One main idea per sentence.
- Avoid the pattern "Students do X, which requires Y, which develops Z." Split it.
- Consecutive very short sentences that are unrelated in content read as abrupt. Reorder or combine where natural flow is lost.

### Specificity Rules
- Name equipment specifically. "Drives" is not specific. "Belt and chain drives" is.
- Do not assume parts have associated drawings. Many IDEA parts are cut from CAD files without formal drawing documentation.
- Do not assume equipment is available without confirming it exists in the IDEA facility.
- Lab activities must be executable by students at the described grade level with the skills they have at that point in the course sequence. Do not describe labs that require skills taught in a later unit.

### Authenticity Markers to Avoid
- "The unit culminates in..." used repeatedly across units.
- "Presentations are evaluated on..." as a closing formula repeated in every unit.
- Identical structural patterns across all unit summaries.
- Corporate jargon replacing plain accurate descriptions.
- Meta-commentary sentences describing what a lab or unit "represents," "demonstrates," or "develops."
- Administrative framing sentences stating something is "the primary assessment."
- Standalone generic safety sentences disconnected from the activity they describe.

### FRC and Competition Terminology
- FIRST Robotics Competition and FRC may be named directly. Supported by approved courses including existing IDEA113 and external approved courses.
- Do not replace FRC terminology with vague substitutes.
- Competition outcomes are acceptable when framed as engineering validation mechanisms.

## 6. COMMON REJECTION TRIGGERS

### Immediate Rejection
- Any unit section under 5 sentences.
- Missing unit lab in an Area D course.
- Unit lab that is a safety orientation, notebook review, or demonstration.
- Missing math co-requisite for Area D course.
- No quantitative analysis in any unit.
- Honors course without a written final examination section.
- Honors course where title field does not include "Honors."

### Likely Revision Request
- Block schedule credit statement missing from overview.
- Course overview does not signal all three Area D UC criteria.
- Honors language appearing in a non-honors course.
- Unit assignment describing multiple disconnected deliverables.
- Unit summary that does not mention mathematical or quantitative reasoning.
- Unit summary that describes assignment logistics rather than student learning.
- Course materials section missing or incomplete.
- Lab with no physical data collection or measurement.
- Lab section describing two separate write-up products.
- Safety language absent from lab sections.

### Extracurricular Flag Risk
- Unit description that reads as club participation rather than academic coursework.
- Tasks listed that are administrative or manual labor rather than engineering.
- No written analytical product associated with a participation-based unit.
- Mitigation: Every unit must have a defined written deliverable. Participation-based units must include quantitative observation and analysis requirements.

## 7. VERIFICATION TOOLS

### Automated Python Check
Run the following script against any course description file to flag sentence length violations, section sentence count violations, and semicolon/colon separator issues:

```python
import re

with open("COURSE_FILE.md", "r") as f:
    content = f.read()

lines = content.split('\n')
violations = []

for line_num, line in enumerate(lines, 1):
    stripped = line.strip()
    if not stripped:
        continue
    if stripped.startswith('#') or stripped.startswith('|') or stripped.startswith('*') or stripped.startswith('-'):
        continue
    sentences = re.split(r'(?<=[.!?])\s+', stripped)
    for sent in sentences:
        sent_clean = sent.strip()
        if not sent_clean:
            continue
        words = len(sent_clean.split())
        if words > 35:
            violations.append((line_num, words, sent_clean[:100]))

print("Violations:", "None." if not violations else "")
for v in violations:
    print(f"  Line {v[0]} | {v[1]} words: {v[2]}")
```

Run this before every submission. Zero violations required before a course goes to the portal.

## 8. BOSCO TECH SPECIFIC CONVENTIONS

### Course Numbering
When a course changes substantially or changes Area, it receives a new number.
- 100-series: IDEA100 (trimester rotations, not UC-approved), IDEA113 (existing), IDEA114 (new)
- 200-series: IDEA205 (archiving), IDEA208 (staying), IDEA209 (new), IDEA210 (new)
- 300-series: IDEA303 (archiving), IDEA304 (archiving), IDEA305 (new), IDEA306 (new)
- 400-series: IDEA401 (archiving), IDEA403 (staying), IDEA404 (new)

### Archiving
Archive old courses at the same time as or before submitting new ones. IDEA304 must be archived before new IDEA304 is submitted to avoid a number conflict.

### Credit Length
All Bosco Tech semester courses award Full Year credit. Submit semester courses as Full Year. Quarter courses (IDEA210) submit as Half Year.

### Prerequisites and IDEA100
IDEA100 has no UC course code. Do not list it as a formal prerequisite. Reference it in the overview as "the school's introductory technology rotation."

### Lab Time Percentages by Course
| Course | Lab % |
|--------|-------|
| IDEA114 | 65% |
| IDEA209 | 65% |
| IDEA210 | 80% |
| IDEA305 | 65% |
| IDEA306 | 75% |
| IDEA404 | 60% |

### Equipment Table Column Headers
Columns are labeled "Equipment" and "Category" in working documents. In the CMP portal these map to "Title" and "Material Type."

### Standards Citation Format
The CMP portal has no dedicated standards field. Standards citations serve as internal compliance verification only and are not entered into the portal. Do not include the standards citation line when entering unit summaries into the portal.

### Portal Entry Format
The CMP takes plain text in each field separately. When entering descriptions into the portal, use no markdown formatting. No asterisks, no bold, no headers inside text boxes. Each section goes into its own field.

### Character Limits
Some CMP text fields have character limits. Paste each section and confirm it is not truncated before saving. If a section is cut, flag it for trimming.
