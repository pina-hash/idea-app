## The finding

Across the 10 committed banks (140 items), the single longest option is the
correct one **79 times, 56.4%**, against 25% at chance. Shuffling cannot help:
length is invariant under permutation, so the tell survives every draw.

The gate is 90% on a short draw, so the rate turns into a pass rate. A student
who knows nothing, always picks the longest option and retakes through the
cooldown clears MDM-2 in about **107.25 attempts**.

**The size of the rewrite: 0 items to shut the gate, 48 to reach chance.**
Those are two different jobs and only the first is urgent -- see "How many items"
below.

## What else leaks, and what does not

"Fires on" is the number of items where the heuristic points at an option at
all; a tie points at nothing and is neither a hit nor a miss. That is why the
longest-option row reads 60.3% of the 131 it fires on, while the headline above
is 56.4% of all 140 items -- the second is the number a student experiences,
because a tie is a question the trick does not answer.

| Dimension | Fires on | Correct | Chance | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Longest option (characters) | 131 | 79 (60.3%) | 25.0% | **the dominant tell** |
| Most words | 106 | 72 (67.9%) | 25.0% | the same tell, second reading |
| Only option with no absolute ("Only...", "Never...") | 9 | 9 (100.0%) | 25.0% | **certain where it fires** |
| Correct option echoes the stem most | 40 | 24 (60.0%) | 25.0% | no signal of its own -- see below |

| Near-duplicate option pair contains the answer | 22 | 12 (54.5%) | 50.0% | noise, not a tell |
| "All / none of the above" | 0 | - | - | clean: none present |

**On the stem-echo row.** Taken alone it looks like a second serious leak: it
fires on 40 items and is right on 24, 60.0% against 25.0%. It is not a
way through the gate. Per bank it fires on at most 14 items and is right on at most
5, below what any bank's threshold needs (MDM-1 5/9, F1 0/8, F2 1/6, F3 2/6, F4 2/6, F5 3/6, MDM-2 3/6, MDM-3 3/6, MDM-9 2/6, MDM-10 3/6),
so a student who answers with the echo where it fires and gets the rest wrong passes
0.0% of attempts on the worst bank -- it cannot clear a unit at any number of retries.
On the 16 items where it and the length tell point at DIFFERENT options, length is
right 8 times and the echo 6; that ratio moves whenever a distractor is lengthened past an
answer that echoes the stem, because the length tell is then wrong on that item by
construction, so it describes the state of the length fix rather than the echo.

**On the other two strategies.** Neither is a second way through the gate, which
is worth stating because if either were, lengthening distractors would not
finish the job.

- **Shortest option** is right on 11 of 140 items (7.9%), well under chance. On
  every bank it reaches fewer correct answers than the pass threshold needs, so
  it cannot clear a single unit at any number of retries. It is the length tell
  seen from the other end, not an independent fact.
- **Most technical-sounding option** fires on 55 items and is right on 31 of them
  (56.4%), which reads alarming, and is 22.1% of the whole corpus, which is
  chance. Where it and the length tell disagree (25 items) length is right
  15 times and this 7. A longer option holds more long words.

## Retaking, which is where the per-attempt numbers stop reassuring

There is **no attempt limit** anywhere in the quiz path. A failure costs a
cooldown -- 60, 300, 900, 3600 seconds, escalating then holding at the last -- and
nothing else. Attempts are independent: each one reshuffles every item index,
and a failed attempt returns a score and the missed topic names, never which
questions were wrong, so nothing accumulates between them and no amount of
memorising the stems helps. That makes the honest question not "what does one
attempt cost" but "how long until it lands".

## How many items have to be rewritten

Two numbers, because they answer two different questions and only one of them
decides whether this happens this week.

- **Shut the gate** is the smallest number of items whose distractors have to
  catch up before a longest-option student passes fewer than 1% of attempts --
  the point at which retrying stops being a route through. Worst items first.
- **Reach chance** is the full job: the longest option is the answer no more

## The rewrite list

Ordered by **give-away**: the correct option's length as a multiple of the
longest distractor offered against it. 1.0 would mean the answer is no longer
than its best distractor and the tell is dead. Items whose answer is not the
longest are not listed -- there are 61 of those, and they are not the problem.

**The fix is to lengthen the distractors, not to shorten the answer.** A correct
option trimmed until it matches is usually a correct option that stopped being
clearly correct.

Each item is listed with **every option and its character count**, because a
rewrite happens at the whole item: the distractor that has to catch up is one of
the three below the answer, and which one is a judgement about the subject.
**Target** is the length the longest distractor has to reach for the tell to
stop firing on this item -- one character past the answer.
