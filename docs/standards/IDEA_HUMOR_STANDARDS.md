# IDEA Humor Standards
**Version 2.1 - 2026-08-25**

Governs deliberate humor in IDEA and Team 5669 presentation artifacts. Owns the humor pass that runs as Step 6b of the scoping protocol in `IDEA_CLAUDE_DESIGN_STANDARDS.md`, against the cooldown record in `IDEA_HUMOR_LEDGER.md`.

**This version is calibrated rather than reasoned.** 1.0 and 1.1 were written from first principles and produced six rounds of rejected output. Everything below derives from actual ratings across those rounds, and where a rule contradicts something that sounds sensible, the rating wins. Audience is Bosco Tech grades 9 to 12. Presenter is Mr. Pina.

Humor is a retention device. A projected deck gets roughly ninety seconds of a fifteen-year-old's attention before decay, and a laugh resets that clock more cheaply than any animation in the token layer. When an insertion does not buy attention back it is cut.

---

## The Three Gates

A candidate passes all three or it is not proposed. They are independent: passing one does not soften another, and the failure record proves it. Midwit and the Pam two-panel both passed Gate 1 cleanly and died on Gate 2 and Gate 3 respectively.

### Gate 1. It must be an argument, not a mood

**Admissible structures:** comparison, dilemma, escalation, sequence, reveal, ranking, refusal.

**Rejected:** any single image conveying a feeling, however well known, however funny in isolation.

The reason is specific to these decks. An IDEA sheet is already a diagram. A structural meme slots in as **content** and is read the way a trade study or a callout is read. A mood image slots in as **decoration**, and decoration inside a deck this disciplined reads as the instructor performing a bit, which is the thing this entire document exists to prevent.

This gate killed the infomercial "better way" cut, which chat had proposed as its single best fit across five rounds and which was rejected on sight. Chat's confidence is not evidence. The gate is.

**Sub-rule: the panels must differ visually.** A multi-panel template whose panels are near-identical faces and whose escalation lives in dialogue is a conversation wearing a diagram's clothes. This is why the Anakin and Padme four-panel rated cringe while the expanding brain, whose panels escalate in visible glow and scale, was the strongest-rated candidate across six rounds.

### Gate 2. Photographic, polished animation, or high-production render

**Rejected outright: the crude web-drawing family.** Wojak, soyjak, chudjak, midwit, party-corner guy, MS Paint line art, any deliberately amateur scrawl. Rejected four separate times across four rounds, twice in explicit terms.

Everything that has ever passed is a photograph, an animation still from a produced film or series, or a rendered figure with real production value. There are no exceptions in the record.

**This is a compositing constraint, not a taste footnote.** Crude foreign line art beside a CAD sheet does not read as deliberate contrast, it reads as sloppy, because the deck has no visual vocabulary that can absorb it. The deep fry works on the opposite principle: it destroys **the deck's own image**, so the room reads intent. Foreign crude art has no provenance to trade on.

**Consequence: a format from the rejected family is available only skinned.** Re-rendering the structure with in-house characters at production quality moves it from fail to pass, and Mr. Pina reached for this independently twice before chat identified it as a rule. Skinning is architecture. See The House Cast.

### Gate 3. Source and register

- **Never require the presenter to speak current slang.** A reference he cannot deliver in his own voice cannot be delivered at all, and he cannot read whether it landed. Killed: 6-7, aura, mogging, Ohio, skibidi, rizz. The last two are worse than stale; they are active negative markers in 2026.
- **Never a property the presenter dislikes.** The Office was rejected on source alone against a clean structure. Chat cannot know these in advance, so every candidate names its underlying source in the humor table and the row can be struck on sight.
- **Anime stills are dead in this room.** Three rejections across three rounds, two from non-recognition. Not proposed again.

## The Currency Method Is Not The Web

**A blog explaining a meme is a death certificate, not a pulse.** Marketing blogs, SEO listicles, and platform discover pages surface a reference at the exact moment it becomes legible to adults, which is the moment it dies with teenagers. Chat searched these in good faith and produced a slate built on 6-7, aura, and mogging, every one rejected as old or cringe, and one the presenter had never entertained at any point.

**Three authorities replace it, in order.**

1. **What Mr. Pina actually hears quoted in the shop and the hallway.** Logged to `IDEA_HUMOR_LEDGER.md` as it happens. The only true signal, and free to collect.
2. **The nostalgia canon,** roughly 2001 to 2016. Permanently legible, zero currency risk, and the only band where the presenter's own reference set and the students' canon overlap, so he deploys from authority instead of from keeping up.
3. **Live testing on one section** before a reference commits to a deck. The three sections run identical pacing, so a reference can be tested in one block and pulled before the other two see it.

**Lead time makes the current band structurally unavailable regardless.** A deck scoped in August ships in October. Anything with a two-to-four week half-life is dead on arrival, and a reference that died two months ago is more damaging than one that is ten years old.

## The Payload Rule

**The template is the vehicle. The payload comes from the room.**

A generic template with generic content earns a polite chuckle, because the room has seen the vehicle a thousand times. Detonation comes from recognition of self: he saw that, he saw **me**. The system's job is therefore not finding better memes. It is harvesting local material and selecting a vehicle for it.

Payload sources in descending yield: a mistake this specific class made, a part that actually failed, a phrase that actually gets said in the shop, the step everybody actually skips, and the instructor's own documented wrong estimate.

A row whose payload could appear in any school's deck is a row that has not been finished.

## Craft: Writing In Their Register

There is exactly one place the presenter may write in student register, and that is **quoted text inside a fake interface**, because quoted is not spoken. A comment section, a chat log, a review. Everywhere else, adult voice.

The rule that makes it work is **specificity over slang.** Real top comments are hyper-specific, technically literate, and deadpan. They are funny for what they notice, not for what vocabulary they use.

Calibrated example, comments under a class CAD render:

```
the origin is just. somewhere.
brother why is it 47 parts
he found the loft tool and it shows
sketch is not fully defined and i can tell from here
sir this is a bracket
he modeled the actual threads. actual threads.
i fear the mates are all coincident
```

Every line is a real critique and none contains a slang term. Generic filler comments are a defect.

## The House Cast

Skinning converts a Gate 2 failure into a pass and solves four problems at once: no rights question, the artifact can leave the building, a recurring character reads as a running bit rather than repetition, and the joke stops being a teacher borrowing a meme and becomes the pathway having a cast.

Priority by rating: the two-buttons operator, Gigachad as the Machinist, Stonks as Number Go Up, and the pointing pair, which is unavailable in any other form.

Generation is the presenter's, on his own schedule. Chat writes render prompts on request only, never unprompted.

**A recurring house character does not trip the reference cooldown**, because it is not a reference. That is the operational advantage and the reason the cast is worth building once.

## Density and Placement

- **There is no per-deck ceiling.** The one-per-five-slides rate and the cap of four are retired as of 2.1. Both were reasoned from a single teardown rather than rated, and the first deck they met was the IDEA209H Unit 1.2 deck, where they would have forced a cut of one of three insertions that each cleared all three gates, sat two or more clean sheets apart, and carried a real local payload. A rule whose first live application is to delete passing work was measuring the wrong thing.
- **What actually governs is spacing and variety, both of which are still hard.** Never two consecutive. Minimum gap of two clean sheets. No structure repeats inside one deck, and no format repeats inside one unit. A deck that trips none of those and still feels like too much is a deck where the insertions are not landing, which is a quality problem the count was standing in for. Zero is legal and is recorded as a spent-nothing row so it is not later read as an oversight.
- **Never two consecutive slides.** Minimum gap of two clean sheets.
- **A chrome-resident gag is one insertion regardless of how many slides it spans**, and is exempt from the consecutive rule since it occupies no slide to stack in. Ceiling of one per deck, or the room reads the footer instead of the sheets.
- **Never on a bookend.** Pathway identity chrome stays straight.
- **Never on a slide carrying a click target.** Those are the sheets walked to the screen for and the presenter is already doing two things.
- **Preferred position is the secondary panel**, under or beside the real claim.
- **Risky tier is the presenter's call, once flagged.** Currently Buff Doge vs Cheems, and the deep fry. Chat names the tier in the humor table so the row can be struck on sight, and does not decide the frequency on his behalf. Still never in a distributed artifact.

### The consequence-slide rule, corrected

1.1 banned humor on any slide carrying a deadline, safety rule, or grade policy, reasoning that adjacent humor makes a consequence look negotiable.

**The correct rule is directional.** Humor that **hardens** a consequence is legal. Humor that **softens** one is not. A threat joke amplifying a deadline reinforces it; a relatable joke about missing deadlines undermines it.

**One absolute carve-out: no weapons, no violence, no implied harm, in any insertion, on any slide, including a threat joke.** Established when a candidate paired a deadline with an armed cartoon and was rejected on sight. Not a taste call, and it does not vary with how funny the result is.

## Remaining Reject Modes

The 1.1 list of eight is superseded by the gates for everything structural. Four survive because the gates do not cover them.

1. **Explained.** A caption or gloss telling the room what is funny. Slides have no timing and no delivery to carry a setup.
2. **Pandering.** The joke is about the audience's identity, habits, or generation. Partly superseded by Gate 3, retained because it also covers content using no slang at all.
3. **Load-bearing.** The sheet's argument depends on the joke landing. Every insertion must be deletable with the sheet still making its point. This is what makes the layer safe to generate automatically.
4. **Aimed at a student.** Absolute, and the part of this mode that never moves. A named student appears only with explicit opt-in, and a joke whose target is a student's work, habit, or result is never proposed at all. Self-directed and room-collective are always fine, and every high-yield format above targets the presenter, the work, the equipment, or the room as a whole.

   **A named adult in the building is Mr. Pina's call, not chat's.** 2.0 read this mode as covering colleagues, administrators, other pathways, and other departments, and the first insertion it met was Mr. Cosso's own Krynen chandelier gag, authored by the colleague it names, running in that colleague's block, and mandated on sight. Chat flags the row as naming an adult and proposes nothing about a named adult that the presenter has not already authorized or authored. He strikes it or he keeps it. What chat still does not do is invent one.

The 1.1 mode "costumed" is retired as wrong: it forbade violating the deck's visual law, which the deep fry does deliberately and successfully. Gate 2 covers what it was actually protecting.

**Prefer the insertion that teaches.** Where two candidates are equally funny, the one carrying a real teaching point wins. It cannot read as decoration, which immunizes it against modes 1, 2, and 3 by construction. Structural load is a liability; pedagogical load is free weight.

## The Humor Pass

Runs as **Step 6b** of the scoping protocol, after the image plan and before aesthetic direction.

Chat proposes one table. Mr. Pina strikes rows. Survivors go into the prompt as authored copy.

| Slide | Structure | Format | Source | Local payload | Exact copy | Asset query | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Structure** names the Gate 1 category. A table where every row is a comparison is a table that has not varied.
- **Source** names the underlying property so a row can be struck on sight per Gate 3.
- **Local payload** states the specific class, part, or failure referenced. A row that could belong to any school's deck is unfinished.
- **Exact copy** is final wording, not a description of the wording.
- **Asset query** is mandatory on every sourced row. A row with no way to find the image is not deliverable.
- **Fallback** is mandatory on every sourced row and must itself be an in-house format.
- **Rows blocked by a cooldown are shown struck through** with the blocking ledger entry named, so the budget is visibly spent rather than silently reduced.

**Gate preflight happens before the table is shown.** A candidate failing any gate is never proposed. Proposing it with a flag moves the judgment back to the instructor, and Block 4 is taught by someone with no Claude access, so an unresolved judgment call is a defect under the Cosso constraint.

## Distribution

**Sourced imagery never leaves the building.** Fine in a room, on a projector, under fair use for teaching. Not fine in a PDF that goes home, on the team site, in a recruiting handout, in an A-G submission, or anywhere public.

A deck whose Step 1 answer includes an export path is scoped in-house and skinned only. A deck acquiring a distribution path after it is built gets its sourced rows swapped for their fallbacks.

---

## Pre-Delivery Checks

Numbered independently of the audit in `IDEA_CLAUDE_DESIGN_STANDARDS.md`. **Never cross-reference the two by number.**

1. Every insertion passes all three gates, checked against the rendered screenshot rather than the table, since Gate 2 failures appear only once the slide exists.
2. Spacing and variety hold: no two consecutive with chrome excepted, a minimum gap of two clean sheets, no structure repeated inside the deck, no format repeated inside the unit. No more than one chrome gag. None on a bookend or an interrogable slide. There is no count to check as of 2.1.
3. Every consequence-adjacent insertion hardens rather than softens, and no insertion anywhere contains weapons, violence, or implied harm.
4. Every insertion is deletable. Remove it in the screenshot review and confirm the sheet still makes its argument.
5. Every sourced row carried an asset query and an in-house fallback.
6. Every row named a local payload.
7. Every delivered insertion, and every one generated and cut, is written to `IDEA_HUMOR_LEDGER.md` in the same pass as delivery.

---

## What Is Automated And What Is Not

**Automated.** Structure selection, gate preflight, placement against the slide inventory, ledger read, cooldown enforcement, local payload sourcing from what chat knows of the course, exact copy including comment-section craft, asset query construction, fallback authoring, ledger write.

**Not automated.** Retrieval of the asset itself. Chat cannot fetch a film still, character art, or a celebrity photograph, and does not pretend otherwise by describing an image it did not see. It supplies a query precise enough to make the lookup a single attempt, and the sheet ships on its fallback if the lookup does not happen.

Skinned house-cast renders are also not automated. Those are the presenter's, on his schedule.
