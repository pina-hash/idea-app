00Why This Exists
Parts pass. Assemblies fail. Height is the one that gets people, because nothing in your part files knows about it. Your body, the hex collar and the shaft above it stack on top of each other, and the collar alone can eat half an inch. A body you measured at 2.80 in on its own becomes a robot over the limit the moment the collar goes on. The assembly is the first place that stack exists anywhere. Find it here, where the fix is a rebuild in SolidWorks instead of a cut part and two days.

The stack, bottom to top
  1. Your body. Whatever height you designed.
  2. The hex collar. Up to 0.50 in of shaft, and most designs use one.
  3. Bare hex above the collar. At least 0.50 in, every time.

All of it together is 3.00 in or under. Run a collar at its full 0.50 in and everything below it has 2.50 in to live in. That is the trade: a taller collar buys you a shorter body, not a taller robot.

02The Limits8 PTS

Measure the assembly, not the parts. Mass comes from Evaluate > Mass Properties, and set the units to grams before you read it. If you are not running a collar, put 0 in that row.
