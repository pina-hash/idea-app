The Course

IDEA is engineering design and manufacturing. Over six weeks you will run a mill, a lathe, a bandsaw, and a belt sander, cut and engrave your own dogtag, design a part that gets 3D printed and then loaded until it breaks, and build an IDEA-Blade with a team to fight in a tournament. You take home the hex core, your dogtag, and your hook. The IDEA-Blade is a team build, not an individual take-home.

This is a sampler. At the end of the rotation you move to a different technology, and at the end of freshman year you choose one pathway for the next four.

Large projects are staged into checkpoints, so a mistake costs the day it happens instead of the whole project. There is no final exam. Letter grades follow the school's scale. This course is not GPA-weighted. Project due dates, the hook competition event, and the Blade tournament fall on different calendar days in each rotation, and all of them are posted in IDEA Classroom.

IDEA Coins are not grade points. The class runs a classroom currency students earn, lose, and spend on things like printing and small privileges. It does not convert into grade points and has no effect on a student's grade in this course. The full rules are in the digital syllabus.

Three Rules That Matter

Deadlines are firm. No extensions. An excused absence moves a due date by the number of days out, and that is the only adjustment. There is typically no homework, but unfinished classwork gets finished outside of class and turned in on time. The shop is open before school from 7:30, at lunch, and after school until 4:30 for exactly that, along with machine time, printing, and help. Machine access outside of class still requires that the student has been trained and cleared on that machine.
Defend it or fix it. A student can be asked to explain any part of what they submitted, in their own words, at any time. Work a student cannot explain is corrected the same day, before it earns credit. That is a correction, not a penalty.
Use AI honestly. AI is allowed. Students verify anything it gives them against the real source and must be able to explain everything they submit in their own words. Every assignment carries a level from OFF to OPEN and asks for disclosure. Most consumer AI services require account holders to be 18 or older, so students should check before signing up for anything.

Shop Safety

Students work on real manufacturing equipment in this course. These rules are not negotiable, and violating them removes a student from the shop.

  • Eye protection any time anyone nearby is cutting, grinding, or machining.
  • Closed-toe shoes. No loose clothing or jewelry. Long hair tied back.
  • No machine runs until the student is trained on it and cleared for that session.
  • Hearing protection where it is called for. Bluetooth headphones and earbuds do not count.
  • Every injury, near miss, and broken tool gets reported immediately. Reporting one is never a problem. Hiding one is.
  • Horseplay is removal from the shop for the day. No warning.
