Launcher: Design

The challenge: your team designs and builds a launcher that throws one FUEL ball as far as it can. Competition is on the football field. Design comes first, on paper, as a team.

Writing: 5 sentences in all Everything else: a pick, a number, a sketch

Before you start

The challenge and the rules

Throw one FUEL ball as far as you can. Three shots, the best one counts.

  • Any launch methodA flywheel or a catapult. Anything else needs your teacher's OK at Sign-off 1.
  • Power: a drillThe shop's 3-speed DeWalt drills by default. The impact driver, another shop power tool, or a power source you build also work. Gearing is your call.
  • Built from metalAluminum extrusion and stock, made on IDEA machines. Wood only with your teacher's OK: this is a mechanism, not a woodshop project.
  • Use what the shop hasAny gears, pulleys, belts and hardware already in the shop. Need something else? Make it from shop stock: printed, routed or lasered. Nothing is bought.
  • Six bearings, topsSix 1/2 in hex bearings per team, signed out and returned after the competition.
  • Full power outdoorsFull-power shots on the football field only. Indoors: low power, aimed flat, never upward.
More rules: testing, the competition
Common senseNever stand in front of a loaded launcher. Hands out of a spinning wheel or a cocked arm.
Test ballsTake as many as you need.
CompetitionOne team fires at a time, in rotation, three rounds. Another team reads where the ball lands on the yard lines. Best shot wins.
AfterThe best one or two launchers stay built. Everyone else returns bearings and parts.

Score every idea

  1. One table for the team. Each sketch gets a letter, A to D, and a column.

Requirements and interfaces

  1. Write one requirement for your part: a number with a unit that a tape measure, scale or test can check.
  2. Type it in your role's box. The green edge marks yours.
  3. As a team, name at least 3 interfaces: the two parts that meet and how they join.

Four requirements, each with a number, and every meeting point written down.

From a bike rack, not a launcher: "Holds 30 lb (13,600 g) with less than 1/8 in of sag, checked with a tape measure." One number, one unit, one way to check it.
